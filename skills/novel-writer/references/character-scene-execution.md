# Character Scene Execution: Core II Prose

Use this reference when an OC must own several prose layers at once: viewpoint narration, attention, action, body, interiority, dialogue, group response, emotional aftermath, and continuation. Use `dialogue.md` or `detail-and-texture.md` alone for narrow defects; this file governs their integration around the person.

## 1. Build the scene runtime

Retrieve a small live packet, not the whole dossier:

```text
identity invariant active now:
experience residue triggered now:
body, fatigue, injury, hunger, intoxication, or recovery:
knowledge, belief, suspicion, and forbidden knowledge:
resource, item, authority, debt, promise, and deadline:
listener models and changed relationship permissions:
unfinished action and recent emotional residue:
live objective, visible alternatives, and first tactic:
```

This is the **scene runtime**. It explains why the same person may sound or move differently today without becoming a different OC. Select one or two active residues; do not make every trait fire in every scene.

For direct continuation, inherit exact position, body, item custody, knowledge, last line, failed tactic, and unresolved pressure. A chapter break does not reset pain, courage, vocabulary, or trust.

## 2. Give narration an owner

Viewpoint ownership affects more than internal monologue. Let it decide:

- what enters attention before the scene's danger or meaning is named;
- what receives an ordinary name, technical category, mistaken label, or no notice;
- which cost appears large, tolerable, shameful, repairable, or someone else's problem;
- how long attention stays on a face, tool, exit, meal, rule, debt, wound, or witness;
- what evidence changes the viewpoint's first interpretation;
- what remains outside awareness.

Use this attention movement:

```text
task-shaped first notice → familiar interpretation → action
→ resistant evidence → reclassification → changed next step
```

The narrator may be more grammatical than the character, but cannot gain the planner's knowledge or a neutral camera's equal coverage. Free indirect style, close third, first person, and limited omniscient can all retain biased categories and priorities.

Do not create voice only through unusual metaphors. Ordinary nouns, order of notice, confidence, omission, and what the person finds worth explaining carry more stable ownership.

## 3. Enter through the character's first tactic

Begin the scene where the person is already trying to preserve or change something. The first tactic should grow from competence, residue, and relationship permission:

```text
ask / check / hide / repair / bargain / prepare / avoid / order / wait / observe
```

Let the world resist materially or socially. Resistance may be weight, distance, law, fatigue, money, another person's separate task, an audience, timing, incomplete knowledge, or the character's own earlier promise.

If the opening action exists only to enter a location, reveal lore, find a clue, or display personality, return to the person's unfinished work and build a reason they would still act if the reader already knew the information.

## 4. Write character-owned action

Action style comes from decision history, not from assigning signature moves.

Before an important movement, know:

```text
target and protected person/object:
distance, route, visibility, timing, and exits:
trained default:
habitual shortcut:
risk accepted first:
risk avoided first:
body and equipment limit:
opponent or environment adaptation:
failure mode characteristic of this person:
```

Two equally skilled people can fight, flee, search, work, investigate, heal, negotiate, or steal differently because they protect different things and price exposure, injury, debt, delay, witnesses, and uncertainty differently.

In combat or emergency scenes, do not force ideal teamwork. Each person begins from their own sightline and task. Temporary coordination occurs when a shouted fact, opened route, fallen object, familiar habit, or visible opportunity becomes usable. Not everyone hears, trusts, understands, or can comply.

Keep physical causality trackable: position, facing, reach, hands, footing, weapon or tool state, injury, resource consumption, changed terrain, and exit. Character ownership cannot excuse spatial impossibility.

After action, distribute consequence unevenly: one counts supplies, one hides a tremor, one blames preparation, one seeks a witness, one resumes the interrupted job. The aftermath completes the action style.

## 5. Write biased interiority and emotional lag

Interiority is the person's current explanation, not the author's diagnosis.

```text
felt pressure → first label the person can tolerate
→ attention or routine changes → action or avoidance
→ contradictory evidence → partial admission, new excuse, or silence
```

Emotion may first alter appetite, spending, route, sleep, work quality, politeness, jokes, risk, touch, counting, cleaning, or delay. Let ordinary obligations continue. A person can cook, file a report, tend a child, attend class, or repair equipment while grief or betrayal changes how those tasks are done.

Use body detail when it interferes with speech, precision, movement, food, sleep, or perception. Avoid cycling through heartbeat, dry mouth, fists, breath, widened eyes, and trembling as free-floating proof of intensity.

Do not require immediate insight or catharsis. A delayed reaction should still leave a trace in the intervening scene. A later realization must grow from accumulated mismatch, not from the chapter needing closure.

## 6. Make dialogue an attempted action

Dialogue begins after the character already has a live target and a model of the listener.

```text
I need you to do / stop / give / believe / overlook ___;
I think you know ___ and will misread ___;
I can order / ask / tease / refuse / conceal ___ with you;
therefore I say ___ first, omit ___, and stop when ___.
```

Let speech compete with physical action. Someone may keep packing while refusing, move the evidence while asking a question, feed a child instead of answering, block a door, call a witness, or finish work to avoid granting the premise.

The next turn responds to what the listener values and permits, not to every logical point. Direct answers remain natural when facts are simple, cooperation is real, or time is short. Partial answers and silence require a recoverable object and a social reason.

Keep vocabulary grounded in age, household, profession, class, region, role, body, and shared life. Do not turn background into jargon display. Sentence length follows purpose, authority, fear, fatigue, and listener—not a universal short-line rule.

## 7. Stage group scenes as intersecting trajectories

Before the shared event, give each important participant one trajectory:

```text
current task or position:
what they control or can take over:
who they watch or avoid:
what they misunderstand:
next move if no one interrupts:
```

When news, danger, a purchase, a revelation, or an arrival enters, assign response by jurisdiction and stake. One person may touch the wound, one secure the door, one continue serving food, one ask about money, one leave to act elsewhere, and one lack permission to do anything yet.

Do not rotate through a cast list. Cross reactions through space and objects: a bowl passed to the wrong person, a chair not yielded, a tool taken back, a name used too formally, a door left open, an instruction ignored. Silence remains legible when the silent person has a task, gaze target, blocked permission, or offscreen next move.

Track unequal knowledge. Group dialogue becomes mechanical when each speaker asks exactly one question from the outline or everyone arrives at the same interpretation together.

## 8. Filter description and information

Description belongs to character runtime when a detail is selected by task, body, residue, avoidance, expertise, or relationship and then changes use or interpretation.

Useful movements:

```text
notice → touch/use → resistance → adjustment
notice → misclassify → consequence → corrected category
ordinary object → changed ownership/condition → relationship meaning
```

World information enters when it changes price, permission, route, danger, work, status, treatment, equipment, or available choice. Let experts speak only within their knowledge and present purpose. Summarize routine teaching or procedure; quote corrections, refusals, personal limits, and words that change the next action.

Do not add equal sensory coverage, profession-themed metaphors, or backstory reminders to prove character. If removing the detail leaves attention, action, and interpretation unchanged, it is optional texture rather than ownership evidence.

## 9. Carry the person across scene cuts

End and hand off with a compact delta:

```text
body and item condition:
new knowledge, false belief, or unresolved suspicion:
resource spent or acquired:
promise, debt, permission, or relationship damage:
strategy used and how the person explains its result:
unfinished action and next likely tactic:
```

The next scene should reveal at least one delta through its first choices rather than recap all six. A wound changes how clothing is handled; a failed apology changes who may touch; spent money removes an easy route; a lie requires a new answer.

Temporary fear, formal register, exhaustion, intoxication, or intimacy does not become a permanent character-card update by itself. Settle durable changes only after acceptance and evidence of persistence.

## 10. Revise without flattening

When a character feels wrong, repair in this order:

1. **truth** — knowledge, body, item, time, position, authority, and continuity;
2. **choice** — identity invariant, visible options, pressure, first tactic, and cost;
3. **relationship** — listener model, permission, shared premise, and changed contract;
4. **scene ownership** — attention, action style, group trajectory, and aftermath;
5. **expression** — oral order, ordinary verb, syntax, detail, paragraph, and tag.

Classify apparent OOC before rewriting: impossible, unsupported convenience, surprising but bridgeable, or earned development/role performance. A missing transition is not fixed by changing the whole personality.

Preserve valid variation. Caregivers, officials, teachers, negotiators, and frightened people may use complete sentences. Emergencies may compress many voices. A private person may confess after earned pressure. Test causes, not surface quotas.

Read the whole scene after repair. Ensure the revision did not make everyone equally terse, witty, messy, eloquent, self-aware, emotional, or competent.

## 11. Calibrate scene scale

| Scene type | Character evidence that matters most |
|---|---|
| Quiet routine | division of work, small permissions, omitted premises, residue changing ordinary tasks |
| Training / instruction | teacher's real topic, learner's bodily misunderstanding, time, repetition, authority, delayed competence |
| Romance / intimacy | approach and withdrawal rights, attention risk, care accepted or refused, shared history |
| Investigation | trained notice, evidence handling, hypothesis bias, authority, witnesses, cost of being wrong |
| Politics / workplace | record, audience, role language, signature, delegation, delay, blame, private versus public permission |
| Combat / chase | sightline, target, footing, equipment, injury, independent tactics, adaptation, exit |
| Grief / recovery | obligation, altered routine, delayed reaction, practical care, failed repair, persistent absence |

Intensity changes the available surface. It does not suspend continuity or make every person react with the same speed.

## 12. Execution tests

1. **Scene runtime** — which invariant, residue, body state, listener model, and unfinished task are active?
2. **Viewpoint** — would another character notice, classify, and omit the same details in the same order?
3. **First tactic** — does the opening action belong to established competence and pressure?
4. **Character-owned action** — do tactics, mistakes, and accepted risks differ for causal reasons?
5. **Interiority** — does the person know more about themselves than evidence supports?
6. **Dialogue** — what response is sought, what is protected, and why this order with this listener?
7. **Group** — who has jurisdiction, who remains on another trajectory, and who cannot respond yet?
8. **Physical truth** — are bodies, objects, positions, time, and costs trackable?
9. **Aftermath** — what changes the next routine, choice, relationship, or resource?
10. **Flattening** — did revision impose one sentence length, emotional style, or competence level?

Stop when the scene belongs to its people. Do not add quirks, gestures, slang, backstory, weather, or psychological explanation after ownership is already clear.
