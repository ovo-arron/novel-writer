## 1. The three-source synthesis

NW inherits three different kinds of useful discipline:

- **Webnovel Writer** contributes long-form continuity, lived scene construction, dialogue ownership, author-feedback learning, and genre-aware serial practice.
- **Humanizer-zh** contributes observable surface symptoms such as inflated significance, formulaic contrast, synonym cycling, false range, and generic conclusion. These are diagnostic clues, never authorship proof or a prose recipe.
- **InkOS** contributes separation among author intent, current focus, canon, plan, draft, audit, revision, and settlement; it also reinforces task-scoped context and non-canonical branch comparison.

Use them in this order:

```text
truth and author intent → character pressure → causal design
→ scene experience → prose and dialogue → audit → accepted settlement
```

Surface humanization cannot repair a false event. A complete outline cannot make an interchangeable character alive. Persistent memory cannot decide what the author has not accepted.

## 2. One living causal chain

Connect character, plot, and prose through one chain:

```text
present task + desired image + feared confirmation
+ habitual strategy + relationship permission + material condition
→ attempt → resistance → unwanted exposure → choice
→ cost → emotional reclassification → residue → next strategy
```

- **Desired image** is how the person wants this listener or group to read them now: useful, harmless, grown, loyal, unhurt, fair, independent, or something else supported by evidence.
- **Feared confirmation** is what they do not want events or another person to prove about them. It need not be a hidden trauma or an explicit thought.
- **Habitual strategy** is the action that has protected both the practical goal and the desired image before. It must have worked often enough to be believable.
- **Unwanted exposure** occurs when resistance reveals the limit, cost, or contradiction of that strategy.
- **Emotional reclassification** means the person changes what the situation or another person now counts as: help becomes pity, caution becomes distrust, a chore becomes exclusion, an enemy becomes a witness. This must alter conduct.
- **Residue** is what survives the scene in body, knowledge, resources, duty, relationship permission, public status, place, or future strategy.

Do not fill every field for every scene. Choose the pressure that makes the current event belong to these people.

## 3. Let character pressure generate plot

Traits describe tendencies; strategies produce events. “Proud, kind, impulsive” is inert until the character tries to protect a practical goal and a self-image at the same time.

For a major character, retain these story-generating fields:

```text
What they want done now:
How they want this person to see them:
What they fear this person or event may confirm:
The strategy that usually protects both:
Where that strategy genuinely works:
Its blind cost under current conditions:
Who resists for an independent reason:
What trouble the character causes while acting reasonably from inside:
What later costly choice would prove a changed priority:
```

The best event is often not the cruelest thing that could happen. It is the consequence of the character choosing the method that has previously kept them safe, respected, loved, or in control.

### Supporting characters

Give each important supporting character only enough independent life to answer:

- What are they already doing before the protagonist enters?
- Which resource, promise, face, relationship, or deadline do they protect?
- What would they do next if the protagonist left?
- Where does their ordinary goal obstruct, redirect, or misread the protagonist?

Do not assign equal dialogue or one subplot to everyone. Independence may appear as absence, lateness, refusal, continued work, or a concern that never joins the main plot.

### Contradiction without randomness

A living contradiction usually has a stable value and changing permission. The same person may be patient with a child, defensive with a rival, and coldly formal with a superior because the possible loss differs. Do not manufacture depth by giving every character a secret opposite personality.

## 4. Let plot become experienced prose

A scene is not fully realized when the planned event happens. The viewpoint must experience a changing model of what is happening.

Use an **attention arc**:

```text
working assumption → selective notice → contact or resistance
→ evidence that matters to this person → reclassification
→ changed action, speech, timing, or omission
```

This is not a mandatory paragraph pattern. It is a check that description, emotion, and action belong to the same movement.

- Detail follows the current task and fear; it does not scan the room evenly.
- Environment changes footing, time, work, comfort, access, speech, cost, or confidence.
- A precise detail earns space when it changes the person's model or the reader's expectation.
- Plain connective sentences carry bodies and objects between high-attention moments.
- Figurative language comes from the viewpoint's available experience and stops after clarifying one perception.
- Dialogue enters where a person tries to alter another person's action, belief, permission, or attention.
- The paragraph may end before interpretation is complete if the next action makes the pressure recoverable.

Beautiful prose is not a separate decorative layer. It is exact selection, material contact, changed interpretation, and controlled rhythm.

## 5. Give sequences and arcs strategy memory

A repeated attempt becomes plot only when the participants remember its cost. Track a compact **strategy-memory delta** after each event:

```text
strategy tried → value it protected → result
→ cost paid → what the character learned, denied, or mislearned
→ how opposition or environment adapted
→ what the next attempt must change
```

Across a sequence:

- do not repeat the same question with a new room or enemy;
- let partial success create permission, evidence, relief, debt, or overconfidence;
- allow a false lesson when it grows from the character's blind spot;
- make the opponent's answer depend on what it observed, lost, or gained;
- carry wounds, money, promises, public knowledge, and damaged trust forward;
- change the method, tolerated cost, relationship reliance, or definition of success.

Across an arc, the proof of change is a later costly choice. The earlier self could understand the option but would not accept its price. Relapse under a different pressure preserves history; it does not erase growth.

## 6. Triangulate reference works without making a collage

Reference works solve different problems. Select two to four only after naming the current craft need.

| Need | Useful mechanism cluster | Tension to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Failure and growth | failure leaves fear, shame, knowledge, relationship damage, and changed coping | do not combine severe residue with effortless universal admiration |
| Building and belonging | protection becomes work, allocation, administration, and unequal obligation | light practical movement must not erase conflict or material cost |
| Action and danger | rule, distance, tool, body, and experience difference alter immediate choices | spatial clarity is not a skill menu or flawless-hero surface |
| Reincarnation as development | body, language, family permission, class, labor, and old flaws obstruct imported knowledge | modern memory is a starting model, not automatic superiority |
| Survival viewpoint | hunger, route, shelter, self-talk, and small promises narrow attention | close comic thought must still return to material choice |
| Institutions and old knowledge | remembered rules collide with lived social reality and group interests | strategic explanation must not become a meeting transcript |

Use `genres-and-works.md` to select lawful comparative works. Then remove work names and pass only one main mechanism plus, at most, one compatible support mechanism into drafting.

State conflicts explicitly. If one mechanism wants fast competence and another wants prolonged bodily learning, choose which owns this sequence. Do not average them into vague “light-novel flavor.”

## 7. Integrated mechanical failures

| Failure | Evidence | Repair first |
|---|---|---|
| Character-card compliance | scenes display traits in turn, but nobody causes trouble by protecting something | restore desired image, feared confirmation, strategy, and independent resistance |
| Pre-solved arc | every failure teaches the exact lesson required for the ending | allow denial, false learning, relationship cost, and opponent adaptation |
| Strategy reset | repeated attempts inherit no body, resource, knowledge, or trust change | write the strategy-memory delta before inventing the next obstacle |
| Supporting-cast orbit | side characters wait to advise, praise, warn, or explain | restore their own tasks, clocks, permissions, and inconvenient choices |
| Decorative prose | atmosphere and micro-actions can be deleted without changing attention or conduct | rebuild the attention arc; remove unsupported decoration |
| Reference salad | recognizable features from several works appear without a shared project pressure | return to one craft problem, name incompatibilities, anonymize the target |
| Perfect contradiction | a character is supplied with balanced flaws and virtues but remains equally articulate everywhere | change relationship permission, practical risk, and what they protect first |
| Structural polish over false causality | scenes are well paced individually but can swap order or viewpoint unchanged | repair inherited entry, changed exit, and the strategy that carries between them |

## 8. Compact execution path

For a broad craft task:

1. Retrieve author intent, canon, current focus, relevant character state, recent residue, and accepted craft evidence.
2. Diagnose the deepest broken link: truth, character pressure, event causality, sequence memory, scene experience, dialogue, or wording.
3. State one character pressure and one operative story question.
4. If direction is uncertain, generate two to four non-canonical branches from different choices; compare cost, character fit, opposition response, and future renewal.
5. Build the chosen event from attempt and counter-action; give it an inherited entry and changed exit.
6. Draft through one viewpoint's attention arc. Use at most one main reference mechanism and one supporting mechanism.
7. Audit the smallest relevant layer. Preserve accepted wording and ordinary prose.
8. Settle only author-confirmed facts, accepted text, and evidence-backed project learning.

For a narrow problem, route directly to `characters.md`, `story-design.md`, `structural-architecture.md`, `detail-and-texture.md`, `dialogue.md`, or `anti-ai-execution.md` instead of running this whole path.

## 9. Tests and stop rule

Use only the tests that match the risk:

- **name-swap:** if another character can perform the scene unchanged, restore decision ownership;
- **relationship-swap:** if the same words and permissions survive a different listener, restore the social risk;
- **order-swap:** if adjacent scenes can trade places, restore inherited state and residue;
- **strategy-memory:** if the next attempt ignores the last cost, rebuild the sequence;
- **attention-deletion:** if atmosphere can vanish without changing notice or action, remove or reconnect it;
- **source-removal:** if the idea becomes generic when reference names disappear, the mechanism was not extracted;
- **old-self test:** state why the earlier character would reject the later choice's price.

Stop when the repaired choice belongs to the character, the next event inherits a changed condition, the prose follows lived attention, and no source surface is reconstructable. Do not continue adding quirks, complications, texture, or anti-AI irregularity after the evidence is gone.
