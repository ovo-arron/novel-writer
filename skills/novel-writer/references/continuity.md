## 1. Separate truth from intention

Maintain distinct conceptual layers even if the author's project uses different filenames.

| Layer | Contains | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Author intent | desired experience, themes, boundaries, long-horizon direction | governs future design, not past facts |
| Canon facts | events that occurred, established rules, identities, relationships, resources | highest factual authority after the manuscript |
| Current focus | what the next one to three chapters should prioritize or postpone | temporary steering |
| Open threads | promises, mysteries, debts, threats, plans, unresolved relationship pressure | obligations and opportunities, not guaranteed payoffs |
| Chapter summaries | compact causal changes and aftermath | retrieval aid, subordinate to manuscript |
| Candidate plans | outlines, forecasts, alternate branches | non-canonical until selected and written |
| Draft prose | proposed events and wording | not automatically canon |

When sources conflict, prefer the author's latest explicit correction, then accepted manuscript text, then canon notes, then summaries, then plans. Report the conflict instead of silently blending incompatible versions.

## 2. Protect what should not be compressed away

### Protected context

- current author intent and prohibitions;
- identities and relationship facts;
- power, resource, and institutional rules currently affecting action;
- recent injuries, promises, debts, secrets, and time limits;
- exact wording the author explicitly asked to preserve;
- open threads due in the current arc;
- current location, time, and available cast.

### Compressible context

- resolved logistics whose consequences are already represented;
- old scene choreography;
- discarded plans;
- repeated descriptions;
- historical details unrelated to current decisions;
- stylistic analysis already converted into a compact target.

Do not equate “mentioned long ago” with “unimportant.” A promise may remain protected because it is still open.

## 3. Retrieve for the task, not for the whole book

Before continuing a chapter, gather only:

1. the last accepted scene or reliable summary;
2. current location and time;
3. present characters, bodies, resources, and knowledge;
4. current focus and chapter objective;
5. open threads that must advance, may advance, or must not resolve yet;
6. genre and voice targets relevant to this scene;
7. author corrections that override older notes.

Loading the entire story bible can make the prose recite its own memory. Relevant facts should constrain choices, not appear merely because they were retrieved.

### 3.1 Immediate scene handoff

For a direct continuation or revision of an unfinished scene, build or read this **required structural slot** before drafting:

```text
Entry action and exact predecessor source:
Time, location, access, and active environment:
Per present character: position/opponent; body/injury; weapon/item custody and condition:
Per present character: knowledge, belief, concealed intent, and immediate want:
Unfinished threats, promises, and actions that must carry across the break:
Unknown or disputed fields that require reopening source:
```

Use exact manuscript passages and current character/item records, not memory of the outline. A weapon changing hands, a wound moving sides, a private intent becoming spoken policy, or an internal label entering prose is a failed handoff. When a field is unknown, mark it unknown and reopen the source; do not fill the gap with the most convenient version. After drafting, compare the exit state against this slot and stage the next handoff in project working state. Routine non-continuous scenes may use only the fields that can materially constrain them.

## 4. Track knowledge per character

For important facts, distinguish:

- objectively true;
- believed by character;
- suspected;
- witnessed but misinterpreted;
- heard from a source of known reliability;
- deliberately concealed;
- forgotten or no longer salient.

Do not give the cast access to the reader's, narrator's, planner's, or other characters' knowledge. Information should spread through plausible contact and leave traces of wording, trust, and distortion.

## 5. Time, travel, body, and resources

Continuity is more than names and lore.

Check:

- elapsed time and sleep;
- route and travel duration;
- weather and seasonal constraints;
- wounds, fatigue, hunger, illness, pregnancy, age, and recovery;
- money, ammunition, medicine, food, tools, transport, and housing;
- messages: who sent them, how they traveled, when they could arrive;
- institutional delays, permissions, records, and witnesses.

A character cannot spend the same coin, reveal the same secret for the first time, or recover from the same wound twice.

## 6. Open-thread states

Use simple states:

- `seeded` — introduced with a recognizable future question;
- `active` — currently changing choices or attention;
- `advanced` — new evidence, cost, or interpretation appeared;
- `deferred` — intentionally postponed with an in-story reason;
- `resolved` — the central question received a causal answer;
- `transformed` — resolved locally but created a new, materially different obligation;
- `abandoned` — author deliberately removed it from the work.

Mentioning a thread is not the same as advancing it. Advancement changes available action, cost, knowledge, or relationship.

Do not pay off every thread on schedule merely to make the outline neat. Avoid bursts where many old promises resolve in one chapter without causal pressure.

## 7. Forecasts and alternate branches

Keep speculative futures isolated from canon. A branch comparison should include its divergence choice, causal beats, benefits, sacrifices, continuity risks, and fit with author intent.

Mark a forecast stale when canon changes one of its required premises. Selecting a branch changes the plan, not the past and not the manuscript until those scenes are written and accepted.

## 8. Chapter settlement

After the author accepts a chapter, record a compact delta:

- facts newly established;
- changes in body, location, resources, status, and relationship;
- knowledge gained, lost, shared, or misinterpreted;
- open threads seeded, advanced, deferred, resolved, transformed, or abandoned;
- promises or deadlines created;
- the emotional or practical residue entering the next chapter;
- any unresolved review warning.

Do not store prose interpretations as facts. “She felt betrayed” is canon only if the text or author establishes it; otherwise store the observable choice and possibly her stated belief.

## 9. Conservative repair

When continuity fails:

1. identify the authoritative source;
2. state the smallest contradiction;
3. determine whether wording, scene order, plan, or canon note is wrong;
4. repair the lowest-authority layer first;
5. preserve unresolved ambiguity if the manuscript intentionally contains it;
6. keep remaining issues visible instead of claiming a complete fix.

Never rewrite several chapters automatically to hide one contradiction.

## 10. Settle across the story-memory domains

Use the compass and eight story domains as retrieval boundaries, not isolated databases:

| Domain | Durable facts |
|---|---|
| Story compass | author intent, reader promise, theme pressure, genre contract, tone, viewpoint, boundaries, and reference mechanisms; guides canon but is not fictional fact |
| Worldbuilding | countries, institutions, law, economy, culture, history, rules, places, and ordinary life |
| Systems | operational rules, terminology, costs, knowledge levels, training, exceptions, and interactions |
| Factions | purpose, decision structure, constituency, resources, internal blocs, operations, alliances, and information state |
| Locations | access, route, distance, layout, current condition, recurring use, jurisdiction, and spatial constraints |
| Events and plot | what happened, causal aftermath, timeline, information spread, plans, and open threads |
| Items | ownership, custody, location, condition, quantity, function, and knowledge |
| Bestiary | range, season, population, behavior, transformation, ecology, and institutional response |
| Characters | identity, wants, beliefs, knowledge, body, resources, relationships, voice, choices, and growth evidence |

After an accepted scene, write one fact in its natural home and link affected entries. Example: a character gives a damaged relic to a temple during a riot. The event owns the transfer action; the item owns new custody and condition; the character owns the promise or loss; the faction owns the temple's decision or new obligation; the world entry changes only if law or wider social practice is newly established.

Before continuing, retrieve by the requested action. A conversation may need two character entries, one recent event, and one item condition—not the entire world. Facts constrain the scene; they do not all deserve mention.

## 11. Hand off to adaptive project memory

Continuity settlement and craft learning are separate operations. A chapter may establish a factual delta without proving a new prose rule.

After accepted work:

1. settle factual changes across the natural story domains;
2. update `working-state.md` with the next practical handoff;
3. pass explicit author feedback and repeated accepted prose evidence through the learning gate in `project-memory.md`;
4. keep unaccepted drafts, branches, and inferred preferences pending;
5. preserve source pointers so compressed memory can be checked against the manuscript.

If no persistent project memory exists or file changes are outside the request, return the proposed delta without claiming it was stored.
