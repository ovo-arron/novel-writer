## 1. Place as usable space

A place is more than atmosphere or a name. It controls access, visibility, movement, work, privacy, danger, status, memory, and cost.

Store climate, settlement patterns, and regional systems in worldbuilding. Store the operational layout, access, route, recurring use, and current condition of a specific place here.

## 2. Location record

```text
Name, aliases, and who uses each name:
Status and source:
Type and larger region:
Scale and boundaries:
Primary uses and users:
Access, ownership, jurisdiction, and restrictions:
Approach routes, travel time, transport, and seasonal changes:
Internal layout or orientation anchors:
Visibility, sound, crowding, light, weather exposure, and escape:
Water, food, heat, sanitation, storage, communication, and shelter:
Economic and social position:
Hazards and maintenance:
Current damage, occupation, closure, or alteration:
Linked characters, factions, events, items, and species:
What different people call or misunderstand about it:
Open uncertainty:
```

Use a sketch or short relational description when exact measurements do not matter.

## 3. Access, distance, and routes

For travel that affects plot, record:

- origin, destination, and intermediate constraints;
- normal duration versus current duration;
- mode, cost, capacity, rest, fuel, food, weather, border, and permit requirements;
- who controls crossings, records passage, or offers shelter;
- message speed compared with body and cargo speed;
- alternate routes and what they sacrifice;
- what travelers know before departure.

Do not teleport characters through unnamed time. Do not over-name ordinary roads. Names arise from local use, administration, history, or real distinctions.

## 4. Scene affordances

Before writing a scene, retrieve only space that can affect action:

- entrances, exits, bottlenecks, obstacles, cover, height, distance, and lines of sight;
- who can overhear, interrupt, arrive, leave, or remain hidden;
- what work is normally performed here;
- what objects are already present for ordinary reasons;
- what social behavior the place permits or forbids;
- what weather, light, noise, crowd, damage, or maintenance changes today.

Do not furnish a room solely with props the scene will use. Ordinary spaces contain habits and inconvenient leftovers.

## 5. Social geography

Places distribute people and power:

- center versus edge;
- public front versus service space;
- safe district versus policed district;
- sacred, gendered, classed, licensed, or forbidden access;
- who commutes, sleeps, waits, sells, hides, governs, cleans, repairs, or is displaced;
- which names appear on official maps and which survive in local speech.

A city is not one mood. Different routes expose different institutions and risks.

## 6. Change over time

Track only changes that persist:

- construction, fire, flood, siege, occupation, migration, quarantine, harvest, festival, curfew, neglect, repair, seasonal closure, and route diversion;
- who performs cleanup and who cannot return;
- how a familiar place is used differently after an event.

Memory may change the viewpoint's attention without physically changing the place. Record the distinction.

## 7. Spatial continuity

Check:

1. Can characters reach the place in the available time?
2. Do they know the route and access rule?
3. Where are bodies, doors, witnesses, tools, hazards, and exits during decisive action?
4. Does weather or damage persist into later use?
5. Are messages, cargo, armies, and refugees moving at plausible rates?
6. Has a discarded draft or map label entered canon?

Precise choreography matters in combat, pursuit, concealment, and locked-room events. Ordinary conversation may need only two or three stable anchors.

## 8. Common failures

- Location is a backdrop that does not touch action.
- Every new scene gets a new proper noun.
- Travel time appears only when delay is convenient.
- A building changes layout to permit an entrance or escape.
- Weather is emotional decoration and never affects work or body.
- A crowded place contains no witnesses, queues, noise, or obstruction.
- A private conversation occurs where privacy is impossible without explanation.
- A battlefield is empty terrain rather than inhabited and supplied ground.
- Cities have one class, accent, occupation, and attitude.
- Destruction leaves no closure, debris, displacement, or repair.

Repair access, use, route, and present condition before adding scenic description.
