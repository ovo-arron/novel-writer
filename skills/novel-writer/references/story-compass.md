# Story Compass

Use this reference to preserve what kind of novel the author is trying to create. The compass is author intent, not fictional-world canon. It guides both ideation and drafting without predetermining every event.

## 1. Compass versus encyclopedia

The compass answers “what experience should this book keep delivering?” The story bible answers “what is true or proposed inside this project?”

Examples:

- “Growth without a cheat ability” is compass intent.
- “The protagonist studied military arithmetic for three years” is character or event canon.
- “War remains serious through civilian and logistical consequences” is a tonal boundary.
- “The northern road takes six days in winter” is location canon.

Do not turn an aspiration into a fact. Do not treat a current plot plan as the only way to satisfy the compass.

## 2. Compact compass record

```text

Reference mechanisms to study:
Surface elements not to copy:
Hard boundaries:
Current uncertainties:
```

Keep this short enough to reread. If it becomes a synopsis, move events to the event-and-plot domain.

## 3. Reader promise

A reader promise describes a recurring experience, not an ending or marketing slogan.

Useful forms:

- Watch a powerless outsider earn belonging while each gain increases divided loyalty.
- Watch cultivation turn material scarcity and family obligation into visible choices.
- Watch competent institutions pursue incompatible futures while civilians live with the delay between orders and reality.
- Watch a relationship change through ordinary cooperation, misreading, and the things neither person can ask directly.

The promise should survive multiple arcs. It may evolve when the author explicitly changes direction.

## 4. Theme as pressure

Do not define theme only as an answer such as “power corrupts.” Define the recurring pressure that lets different characters produce different answers:

```text
When protection requires control, what does each person sacrifice first?
When belonging demands complicity, what counts as betrayal?
When knowledge creates advantage, who is entitled to use it?
```

Events test the question. Characters do not need to state it. Opposing answers may remain partly valid.

## 5. Genre and narrative contract

Record what readers may reasonably expect and how this project differs:

- progression visibility and pace;
- mystery fairness and information access;
- romance centrality and relationship exclusivity;
- comic density and whether danger retains cost;
- violence, death, recovery, and reversibility;
- scale: household, school, sect, city, state, war, or world;
- release rhythm when serialized: daily life, goals, reversals, aftermath, and installment settlement;
- use or rejection of cheats, systems, reincarnation knowledge, prophecies, and chosen-one logic.

Genre expectations are tools, not obligations to copy familiar sequences.

## 6. Tone, intensity, and ethical distance

Define ranges rather than a single adjective:

- playful ↔ solemn;
- intimate ↔ panoramic;
- hopeful ↔ tragic;
- restrained ↔ graphic;
- ironic ↔ sincere;
- fast consequence ↔ delayed consequence.

State how the project treats suffering. Seriousness can come from interruption, labor, complicity, recovery, and unequal protection—not only explicit violence. Comedy may coexist with danger without resetting its cost.

## 7. Character and viewpoint promise

Record only what applies:

- whose change, endurance, failure, relationship, investigation, or perception anchors the book;
- whether viewpoint is close, rotating, retrospective, unreliable, or panoramic;
- what kinds of knowledge the narrator cannot provide;
- how much self-understanding characters normally possess;
- whether the book values ensemble autonomy or protagonist centrality;
- what would count as an OOC shortcut against the intended development.

Viewpoint is a promise about access. It is not permission to hide information a viewpoint character plainly recognizes.

## 8. Reference influences

For each named work, preserve only:

```text
Dimension studied:
Transferable mechanism:
Why it fits this project:
What must remain different:
Evidence source and confidence:
```

Use several works for different dimensions. A cultivation work may inform progression visibility, a political fantasy may inform institutions, and a relationship novel may inform dialogue. Do not ask one title to supply the entire book.

Remove work and author names from the final drafting brief. Keep names only in research notes.

## 9. Boundaries and uncertainty

Boundaries may include:

- unwanted themes, relationship structures, content intensity, or protagonist behavior;
- facts the author does not want decided yet;
- liked wording, voices, or scenes that revisions should protect;
- real-world cultures or histories requiring research before use;
- commercial or platform constraints only when the author asks to consider them.

An uncertainty is not a blank to fill automatically. Generate labeled alternatives and state their tradeoffs.

## 10. Compass audit

Before accepting a major direction, ask:

1. Does it renew the reader promise or replace the book with a different attraction?
2. Does it pressure the theme rather than state an answer?
3. Is the genre expectation being used, varied, or intentionally refused?
4. Does the direction respect viewpoint and the project's chosen change, endurance, or failure principle?
5. Does it violate a boundary or silently resolve an uncertainty?
6. Can the idea survive after reference names and surface features are removed?
7. Does it create several future choices, not only one spectacular scene?

Update the compass only when author intent changes. Do not revise it merely to justify a convenient plot.
