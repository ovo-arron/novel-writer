# Evidence-Based Review and Revision

Use this reference to diagnose, review, polish, or revise fiction. Review is not an excuse to standardize prose. Preserve effective plain language, character-owned roughness, deliberate formality, and quiet scenes. When the author's diagnosis is broadly “AI/mechanical,” use `anti-ai-execution.md` to select the failed layer before applying this revision procedure. When narration, action, interiority, dialogue, group response, and aftermath all lose the same character ownership, use `character-scene-execution.md` before local polishing.

## 1. Diagnose the level before changing text

| Level | Typical evidence | Appropriate action |
|---|---|---|
| Word or sentence | unnatural collocation, redundant explanation, unclear object | local rewrite |
| Dialogue exchange | shared abstraction, question-answer relay, universal wit | repair turn order and speaker ownership |
| Paragraph | repeated meaning, uniform rhythm, detached description | restructure the paragraph |
| Scene | no motivated activity, lesson exists only for lore, conflict is spoken not enacted | rebuild action and information order |
| Chapter | no causal movement, climax solves a different problem, aftermath missing | revise chapter plan |
| Story or continuity | motive, rule, time, knowledge, or resource contradiction | reconcile against canon and author intent |

Do not solve a scene problem by replacing adjectives. Do not redesign a story because one sentence is stiff.

## 2. Review dimensions

Review only dimensions relevant to the request, but keep them conceptually separate.

### Story causality

- Do actions follow motives, knowledge, and available resources?
- Does opposition respond rather than wait for the protagonist?
- Does the result change later options or costs?
- Is a twist earned by prior conditions rather than inserted surprise?

### Worldbuilding

- Do government, law, economy, class, culture, geography, magic, and technology constrain action where relevant?
- Is the text confusing objective fact with propaganda, belief, rumor, or local custom?
- Does a major new rule create plausible ordinary and institutional consequences?

### Systems, factions, and locations

- Do magic, cultivation, technology, medicine, language, time, and rank retain established inputs, limits, knowledge levels, and exceptions?
- Do organizations act through plausible authority, resources, information, procedures, and internal interests?
- Are access, route, travel time, layout, witnesses, weather, damage, and spatial positions consistent where they affect action?

### Events and plot

- Are world chronology, reader revelation, and character knowledge kept distinct?
- Did a planned, rumored, or alternate event accidentally become canon?
- Do scenes carry unfinished action, cost, or relationship pressure into one another rather than fill separate outline slots?

### Character ownership

- Could another character inherit the same observations, lines, and choices unchanged?
- Does biography leave a learned rule, trigger, first move, blind cost, or relationship habit in the present scene?
- Does competence have a boundary?
- Does emotion alter behavior?
- Do relationships change what can be asked, refused, or left unsaid?
- Does any decisive act fail the OOC gate: supported want, belief, permission, pressure, prior evidence, and lasting residue?
- Before rewriting an apparent breach, is it impossible, unsupported convenience, surprising but bridgeable, or earned development/role performance?

### Scene necessity

- What disappears if this scene is cut?
- Would the characters still perform this activity if the reader already knew the lore?
- Does the setting physically or socially affect the action?

### Prose

- Are meanings repeatedly completed after the evidence already landed?
- Do paragraphs converge on the same rhythm or moral finish?
- Are abstract evaluations replacing specific work, cost, or consequence?
- Has “humanization” made everything short, casual, messy, or slang-heavy?
- Are details selected by viewpoint and present action, or added as an even visual and sensory coating?
- Does one exact image or material trace have room to land, or has every sentence been polished to compete with it?

### Dialogue

- Do several people repeatedly use premise–reason–conclusion speech?
- Are abstraction, psychological insight, and information access implausibly shared?
- Does every turn answer, counter, and advance with equal efficiency?
- Are actions attached to every line without causal work?

### Continuity

- Who knows what, and from where?
- Are time, body, travel, resources, names, and rules consistent?
- Did a draft or discarded plan accidentally become canon?

### Items and living systems

- Are ownership, custody, location, condition, quantity, and knowledge of important items consistent?
- Do artifacts retain established limits, maintenance, legal, economic, and political consequences?
- Do recurring organisms, plants, spirits, diseases, or anomalies retain habitat, season, behavior, population, and aftermath?

## 3. Evidence threshold and issue budget

Report a prose pattern only when it repeats or when one occurrence damages a pivotal moment. Quote a short local phrase or identify the exact turn pattern. Explain the reading effect and propose the smallest repair.

Default budget:

- at most three high-impact craft categories per pass;
- at most one dialogue category;
- continuity and factual contradictions may exceed the budget when they block comprehension or future chapters;
- absence of a technique is not an issue by itself.

Do not call text “AI-like” without observable evidence. Do not use an AI detector score as literary proof.

## 4. Author-feedback contract

When the author rejects or likes specific wording:

1. Treat the reaction as evidence about timing, speaker ownership, relationship distance, age, scene state, and unintended facts.
2. Preserve accepted time, place, props, actions, relationships, resources, uncertainty, and liked wording when facts allow.
3. Do not merely substitute synonyms, move the rejected implication to another speaker, or invent a stronger event to justify it.
4. Transfer the mechanism of a liked line—task-grounded vocabulary, relationship permission, shared premise, incomplete explanation, or unforced timing—not its sentence shell.
5. Revision does not mean approval. Keep draft and accepted states distinct when the project does.
6. If the author rejects an activity itself, do not rename or cosmetically humble it. Run the deletion and swap tests, then rebuild the causal day if necessary.
7. If an entire exchange must be rewritten, remove its dialogue and recover the visible actions, objects, and consequences first. Restore only the turns the new scene requires.

### Interpret the rejected layer

An author correction is not automatically a word ban. Locate the deepest layer the feedback actually rejects:

1. **word or collocation** — the verb, object, measure, or spoken chunk is not idiomatic here;
2. **spoken turn** — the line answers the wrong pressure, exceeds relationship permission, or uses knowledge the speaker lacks;
3. **action causality** — the body, object, weapon, or attention changes without a sufficient trigger;
4. **scene activity** — the character is performing a task invented only to enter a location, deliver lore, expose a clue, or display competence;
5. **continuity or story logic** — time, custody, motive, consequence, or established fact is broken.

Repair the deepest failed layer first. If changing the noun, setting, or terminology leaves the same people doing the same thing for the same author convenience, the correction has not been learned.

For reusable learning, separate five fields:

```text
surface evidence → rejected layer → observable reason
→ next-use test → counterexample or boundary
```

The observable reason may be oral collocation, relationship permission, knowledge boundary, physical causality, scene necessity, or continuity. Do not claim access to the author's hidden taste; infer only what the feedback and accepted alternative support.

## 5. Revision procedure

### Pass A: truth

Verify events, motives, knowledge, time, location, body, resources, author constraints, world and system rules, faction decisions, spatial access, item custody, and living-system conditions. Run the OOC gate on decisive behavior. Do not polish a contradiction.

### Pass B: scene

Check entry state, pressure, attempt, resistance, choice, changed exit state, and residue. These need not appear as equal beats, but the causal movement must exist.

### Pass C: expression

Choose the highest-impact prose or dialogue evidence. Repair local passages without running a global search-and-replace.

### Pass D: regression

Reread at least the surrounding three paragraphs or the whole short scene. Check that the fix did not:

- erase a character difference;
- introduce a new fact;
- make all sentences share one length;
- turn ambiguity into confusion;
- move necessary information too far away;
- add a theme summary;
- weaken pacing by preserving every explanation.

## 6. Common bad revisions

- Removing every connector, dash, adverb, complete sentence, or abstract noun.
- Adding stutters, slang, profanity, ellipses, fragments, sensory clutter, or random failure as “human texture.”
- Expanding every noun into color, texture, smell, metaphor, and symbolic meaning in the name of delicacy.
- Replacing precise ordinary words with picturesque ones.
- Making all dialogue shorter.
- Adding an action tag after each spoken line.
- Turning all subtext into deliberate evasion.
- Breaking a strong causal scene merely because its structure is clear.
- Preserving a rejected scene function under new props and terminology.
- Explaining why the new version is emotional inside the manuscript.

## 7. A/B comparison rubric

When comparing two versions, ask for concrete evidence:

1. Which version gives the viewpoint character clearer ownership of attention?
2. Which version makes action follow motive, knowledge, and relationship more credibly?
3. Which version preserves necessary information with less authorial explanation?
4. Which dialogue sounds different because the people differ, not because one version added slang?
5. Which ending follows from what happened rather than summarizing it?
6. Which revision introduces fewer new facts and fewer unintended implications?

“More natural” is a conclusion, not evidence.

## 8. Review output

Unless the author requests another format, report each issue as:

```text
Location:
Level:
Evidence:
Why it matters:
Smallest repair:
```

If no actionable evidence exists, say the text passes the requested review. Do not invent issues to fill a quota.
