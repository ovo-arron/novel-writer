## 1. Scope and relevance

Not every bird or weed needs an entry. Use three levels:

- **background** — establishes climate, season, food, sound, or local routine;
- **recurring** — repeatedly affects labor, travel, health, trade, belief, or danger;
- **pivotal** — materially changes plot, politics, ecology, identity, or character choice.

Only recurring and pivotal entities need durable records. A bestiary is a living-world tool, not a combat catalog.

Do not fill unknowable biology for a ghost merely to complete a form. Replace biology with the conditions that sustain, summon, disperse, transform, or recur.

## 3. Ecology and life cycle

For a recurring species, ask:

- What does it consume, compete with, pollinate, infect, prey on, shelter, or decompose?
- What limits its population?
- What signs does it leave before direct appearance?
- How do weather, season, migration, war, settlement, magic, and trade change its range?
- What happens when people hunt, farm, domesticate, eradicate, worship, or relocate it?
- Which other organisms depend on it?

An apex monster cannot exist as an endless encounter generator without food, territory, reproduction or recurrence, and pressure on surrounding settlement.

Plants and fungi deserve action roles beyond scenery: food, medicine, poison, fuel, fiber, construction, soil, ritual, invasive spread, seasonal work, trade, and disputed knowledge.

## 4. Intelligence, culture, and personhood

If an entity can reason or communicate, distinguish:

- individual intelligence from species stereotype;
- instinct, learned behavior, custom, law, and religion;
- language capacity and translation access;
- kinship and social organization;
- recognized or denied legal personhood;
- internal disagreement;
- what humans or other peoples falsely believe about it.

Do not make every member morally or intellectually identical. “Monster,” “demon,” “spirit,” and “beast” may be political categories rather than neutral biology.

## 5. Spirits, undead, constructs, and anomalies

For non-biological entities, define operational conditions:

- origin or creation;
- anchor, territory, vessel, command, memory, or unfinished condition;
- perception and communication;
- persistence and recurrence;
- change through attention, ritual, damage, naming, law, or environment;
- what counts as injury, death, release, corruption, repair, or reproduction;
- competing scholarly, religious, and popular explanations.

Keep uncertainty when the story has not proven one theory.

## 6. Human and institutional relations

Connect important entities to:

- agriculture, medicine, craft, food, transport, waste, and disease;
- hunting, conservation, domestication, military use, and pest control;
- religion, folklore, taboo, naming, and funeral practice;
- law, ownership, trade, quarantine, and borders;
- class: who receives protection, who performs dangerous work, who profits;
- war and displacement;
- items made from, for, or against the entity.

Local people may know more accurate practical facts than scholars while holding less accurate theories.

## 7. Encounter design

Before an encounter, establish:

1. why the entity is here now;
2. what it is doing independent of the protagonist;
3. what signs are available;
4. what the viewpoint character knows or mistakes;
5. terrain, distance, weather, body, tools, and escape routes;
6. what the entity protects, seeks, avoids, or cannot perceive;
7. what changes if characters kill, flee, feed, bargain with, capture, cure, or ignore it.

An encounter may be husbandry, harvesting, infection, migration, haunting, negotiation, rescue, observation, or cleanup—not only combat.

## 8. Continuity and common failures

Track range, season, population, wounds, transformation, known signs, and institutional response when they persist.

Common failures:

- Species exists only when a fight is scheduled.
- Ecology is a decorative food-chain paragraph with no effect on people.
- Plants are scenery and animals are enemies.
- Every creature has a game-like skill list.
- A rare species appears wherever convenient without route or cause.
- Characters instantly know danger level and weaknesses.
- One specimen proves every member behaves alike.
- Killing a major predator, spirit, or plague source changes nothing afterward.
- A sentient people is reduced to a single culture and moral alignment.
- Scientific, religious, and local explanations are silently blended into objective fact.

Repair habitat, motive, evidence, and aftermath before inventing another ability.
