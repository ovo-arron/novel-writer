# Characters, OC, Voice, and OOC Control

Use this reference to create, organize, query, continue, or repair characters. Character fidelity is the highest-priority constraint during drafting: events may pressure a character into surprising behavior, but the manuscript must earn the change through knowledge, relationship, cost, and choice. Read `character-architecture.md` for new OCs, cast ecology, relationship arcs, competence design, moral escalation, and character-generated opposition. Read `character-scene-execution.md` when the character must own viewpoint, action, dialogue, group response, aftermath, or a direct continuation. Use `character-authenticity.md` for biography-to-behavior and OOC diagnosis.

## 1. Character is a decision system

Do not define an OC only through adjectives, biography, appearance, or trauma. Build a recognizable decision pattern:

```text
want + values + knowledge + competence + blind spot
+ relationship permission + body/resources + present pressure
→ choice → cost → remembered change
```

The same person can behave differently with a parent, rival, subordinate, child, stranger, or enemy without becoming inconsistent. Fidelity lies in why the behavior changes.

## 2. Character record

Use a compact core, then add only story-relevant depth:

```text
Name, aliases, age, identity, and current role:
Status and source:
Visible want:
Private need or fear of what others may confirm:
Values and priority order under pressure:
Lines they resist crossing, and known exceptions:
Competence and earned authority:
Blind spots and false beliefs:
Social mask and what threatens it:
Default pressure response: control / appease / flee / joke / bargain / freeze / overwork / other
What they protect first when time runs out:
Current body, resources, duties, and public status:
Key relationships and asymmetries:
Secrets: fact, holder, concealment method, and exposure risk
Current arc pressure:
Growth test: a future choice that would prove change
Open uncertainty:
```

Do not invent a secret, trauma, contradiction, or quirk merely because the card has a field.

## 2A. Character pressure engine

Use the record to produce choices, not to display traits. For a character carrying an event, specify only what current evidence supports:

```text
Desired image with this person or group:
Feared confirmation:
Practical goal now:
Habitual strategy that protects goal and image:
Where the strategy has worked before:
Blind cost under present conditions:
Independent person or condition that resists it:
Trouble the character causes while acting reasonably from inside:
Later costly choice that would prove a changed priority:
```

`Feared confirmation` is not a compulsory secret trauma. It may be as ordinary as being treated as a child, debtor, burden, coward, outsider, incompetent worker, unreliable parent, or disloyal friend. Do not name it if the manuscript has not earned it; pressure may remain visible through what the person protects, explains, hides, or tries to finish.

A plot-bearing character should cause at least one consequence through a strategy that previously made sense. If the event still occurs unchanged after removing the person's desired image, relationship permission, and blind cost, the outline is using the character as transport.

Supporting characters need independent continuation, not equal subplot weight. Record what they were doing before the protagonist arrived, what they protect, and what they would do next if the protagonist left. Absence, lateness, refusal, divided attention, and continued work can establish independence more naturally than an assigned speech.

## 2B. Experience residue and runtime state

A biography matters only when it leaves present evidence. Convert a story-relevant experience through this chain:

```text
experience → learned rule → present trigger → automatic first move
→ boundary or exception → cost → current evidence
```

The learned rule is the character's conclusion, not objective truth. It may once have protected them and now misprice danger, help, shame, debt, or trust. Do not require a flashback whenever it fires.

Draft the person as a runtime state rather than a frozen card:

```text
identity invariant + relationship mode + experience residue
+ current body/resources/knowledge + live task + recent emotional residue
→ available behavior now
```

An invariant is a durable priority or decision tendency, not a fixed sentence length. Injury, exhaustion, fear, intimacy, public role, and concealment may change the surface while the person still protects the same thing. Read `character-authenticity.md` for the full biography-to-choice and OOC method.

## 3. Voice record

Voice is what a person notices, asks for, refuses, assumes, and risks—not a bag of catchphrases.

Record:

- **vocabulary sources**: household, region, age, school, trade, military, faith, class, illness, hobbies, or bureaucracy;
- **attention habits**: money, exits, injuries, status, food, rules, faces, tools, fairness, mistakes, or future risk;
- **oral order**: whether requests, answers, refusals, warnings, or reasons tend to come first;
- **explanation threshold**: when this person explains fully, minimally, defensively, professionally, or not at all;
- **face-saving method**: joke, anger, precision, silence, counter-question, practical help, retreat, or blame;
- **uncertainty language**: how they guess, hedge, deny, or ask for proof;
- **emotion leakage**: which choices, routines, errors, or topics change before they admit a feeling;
- **register range**: public, professional, intimate, frightened, exhausted, and angry speech;
- **rare surface habits**: catchphrases, dialect, profanity, stutter, honorifics, or verbal tics only when established by the manuscript.

One strong relationship-conditioned pattern is more useful than ten decorative mannerisms.

## 4. Relationship-specific behavior

For each important pair, record directionally; A toward B may differ from B toward A:

```text
Public relationship:
Private dependence:
Power and permission: who may order, interrupt, touch, refuse, tease, or leave
Shared history and shared premises:
What each wants from the other now:
What each misreads:
Forbidden or dangerous topics:
Typical repair after conflict:
Current unresolved pressure:
```

Update the relationship when behavior or obligations change, not whenever characters share a scene.

## 4A. Listener model and microhistory

Permission alone is insufficient. Before an important turn, determine the speaker's **listener model**:

- what the speaker thinks this listener already knows;
- what the listener will accept, challenge, misread, repeat, or use against them;
- what the speaker wants changed and what must remain hidden;
- how much explanation, interruption, touch, teasing, refusal, or departure this relationship permits.

Carry compact microhistory when it changes the turn: unpaid favor, broken promise, usual division of work, old embarrassment, form of address, touch rights, the last failed repair, or a subject both avoid. The two people may remember the same history differently. Do not make them recap it for the reader.

## 5. Knowledge, body, and resources

Before using a character in a scene, retrieve:

- what they know, believe, suspect, misunderstand, conceal, or forgot;
- how they learned it and how reliable the source seems to them;
- present injury, fatigue, hunger, age, training, disability, and recovery;
- money, equipment, authority, allies, transport, and time;
- promises, debts, duties, fear, and recent emotional residue;
- current location and plausible route.

Do not give a character the planner's knowledge or a competence they have not earned.

## 6. OOC gate before drafting

For every key character in the requested scene, write an internal one-line state:

```text
Now wants ___; believes ___; can/cannot ___ with this listener;
under pressure from ___; carries residue from ___.
```

Then test the planned decisive action:

1. Does the character have the knowledge and ability?
2. Does the choice serve a want, value, relationship, fear, duty, or pressure response already supported?
3. Why this choice now rather than the easiest alternative?
4. What does the choice cost socially, materially, bodily, or morally?
5. What prior scene, habit, or present trigger earns any surprising departure?
6. What residue will make the departure part of character history rather than a convenient exception?

If the answer depends only on “the plot needs it,” redesign the event. Do not hide OOC behavior beneath eloquent inner analysis.

## 7. Growth versus OOC

Growth is not perfect consistency. It is change with memory.

A credible change usually contains several of these, not necessarily all:

- repeated pressure on an old strategy;
- evidence the old strategy no longer protects what the character values;
- failed or partial alternatives;
- another person's influence that the character resists or misuses first;
- a costly choice;
- awkward behavior after the choice;
- relapse under different pressure;
- a later choice demonstrating a new priority without announcing it.

Sudden behavior can be valid when caused by panic, intoxication, coercion, illness, grief, revelation, role performance, or a deliberately hidden side. Establish the cause and preserve aftermath. Surprise is not the same as OOC; unsupported convenience is.

## 8. Dialogue and behavior verification

For speech, verify in this order:

1. Does the person have a reason to speak now?
2. What do they want the listener to do, believe, stop, give, forgive, or fail to notice?
3. What can they say to this listener without losing face or power?
4. Which words come from their life rather than the author's outline?
5. What do they leave assumed, misunderstand, or refuse?
6. Is the sentence length supported by role and purpose?

For action, ask what the person notices first, what shortcut they prefer, what they protect, and where their competence stops.

Do not enforce difference by making every character eccentric. Plain people still differ in attention, permission, knowledge, and stakes.

## 9. Character continuity and repair

After an accepted scene, update only durable deltas:

- new knowledge or changed belief;
- promise, debt, injury, possession, role, status, or relationship change;
- choice that confirms or challenges an established pattern;
- new behavior repeated enough to become a voice or habit anchor;
- author correction or liked wording that should remain protected.

When a passage feels OOC, classify it before repairing:

- **Impossible** — the person lacks required knowledge, ability, access, body, time, or resources. Change the action or conditions.
- **Unsupported convenience** — the act exists for the plot and has no supported value, pressure, strategy, or aftermath. Redesign it.
- **Surprising but bridgeable** — the act can grow from existing identity, but a trigger, blocked alternative, escalation, or transition is missing. Add only that bridge.
- **Earned development or role performance** — earlier pressure, cost, rehearsal, concealment, or social role supports the departure, and residue persists. Keep it.

Then:

1. identify the exact line or action;
2. compare it with current state and earlier evidence;
3. decide whether the error is knowledge, motive, relationship permission, voice, body/resource, or missing transition;
4. repair the smallest layer;
5. if the author wants the surprising act, add the necessary pressure or earlier preparation without rewriting the person's entire identity.

## 10. Common failures

- Adjective card substitutes for behavior.
- Trauma explains every decision.
- Intelligence means accurate insight into everyone.
- All smart characters share the author's abstractions.
- Character difference is only slang, catchphrase, sentence length, or profanity.
- A normally private character gives a complete confession because the chapter needs closure.
- A caring character becomes a therapist and diagnoses motives perfectly.
- A rival waits for the protagonist instead of pursuing a future.
- Growth is stated in a monologue rather than tested through choice.
- A sudden betrayal, romance, cruelty, forgiveness, or sacrifice has no pressure history.
- Revision makes dialogue smoother while erasing relationship permission.
- One accepted line becomes a repeated template for every scene.

Protect the person's decision logic first. Polish wording only after the choice belongs to them.
