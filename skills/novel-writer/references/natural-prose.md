# Natural Prose and Anti-Mechanical Writing

Use this reference while drafting prose or repairing text that feels generic, over-polished, promotional, overly thematic, or mechanically “humanized.” If the author gives a broad “AI/mechanical” diagnosis, use `anti-ai-execution.md` first and return here only for the matched prose symptom. The target is not disorder. The target is prose with a viewpoint owner, material resistance, uneven knowledge, and consequences.

## 1. Positive prose shape

### Let attention choose the detail

Do not scan a room evenly. A hungry person notices portions and smell; an injured person notices distance and places to sit; a liar notices who is eager to believe; an apprentice notices wear on tools. Description becomes voice before the narrator makes any clever comment.

### Give narration a partial stance

Even restrained narration selects, compares, dismisses, lingers, or misunderstands through a person and situation. Use a **partial stance**: what feels normal, irritating, impressive, embarrassing, suspicious, or not worth naming to this viewpoint. Do not manufacture opinion in every sentence; neutral description is natural when attention is on the task. The problem is frictionless comprehensiveness, not calm prose.

Specificity must come from canon, observation, or the scene's established material. Do not replace a vague sentence with invented specificity—fabricated dates, brands, injuries, statistics, memories, or sensory facts are still false. When the source is uncertain, preserve the uncertainty or ask; humanization is not permission to counterfeit evidence.

### Let sentences follow thought and work

A sentence group may move through:

```text
notice → assume → touch or act → discover a mismatch → revise
```

It may also remain plain:

```text
do the work → encounter friction → adjust
```

Do not make every paragraph complete “perception → interpretation → meaning → theme.” Stop when the character would move on.

### Make emotion alter conduct

Fear is not automatically trembling. It may choose the longer road, pay too much, delay a question, hide useful information, volunteer first, or refuse to go home. Anger may make a person overly polite. Grief may preserve an ordinary routine because abandoning it would make the loss real.

### Let objects remain objects

Food can be eaten, tools can be inconvenient, clothes can be wet, and a gift can appear once. An object may later gain narrative meaning, but it does not need to serve life, plot, symbol, foreshadowing, and relationship at the same time.

### Leave aftermath in the world

After danger or revelation, allow fatigue, cleanup, changed schedules, missing supplies, awkward obligations, misremembered details, or administrative consequences. Do not compress consequence into one beautiful sentence and reset the cast.

## 2. Compact recurring screen

During a first draft, choose at most one relevant risk. After the draft, scan the whole scene once.

1. **Characters live before they perform plot** — meals, work, injury, money, promises, and relationships may drive the scene.
2. **Objects have ordinary use before narrative use** — do not invent tutorial materials or symbolic props just to demonstrate a rule.
3. **Information is not passed in a relay** — each person says only what they know, want, and are permitted to say.
4. **Daily life is not automatic detective work** — noticing does not require lists of suspects, evidence ledgers, or a spoken deduction.
5. **Training is not a tutorial level** — avoid default “fail once → rule explained → instant success.”
6. **Environment touches action** — weather, time, terrain, and architecture matter through clothing, work, travel, fire, body, or cost, not a five-sense quota.
7. **End on what happened** — a choice, unfinished task, refusal, arrival, altered object, or quiet continuation can close the scene without a thesis.
8. **Names come from use** — do not coin pseudo-specialized roads, institutions, exercises, tools, or magic terms merely to make the world sound designed.
9. **Connectors do not argue for the character** — words such as “therefore,” “at least,” “instead,” and “as long as” are normal, but recurring chains can expose an author completing the reasoning for everyone.
10. **Ordinary prose is allowed** — not every transition needs texture, wit, metaphor, or a memorable last line.

## 3. General AI-text symptoms adapted for fiction

These patterns draw on general humanization research but must be judged in context. A single word or construction is never sufficient evidence.

| Symptom | Fictional manifestation | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Inflated significance | A cup, glance, or door immediately “marks a turning point” | Keep the physical event; let later behavior prove importance |
| Promotional language | Worlds, powers, cities, and characters are “breathtaking,” “vibrant,” or “unparalleled” before earning interest | Replace evaluation with specific use, cost, damage, or social response |
| Shallow appended analysis | An action is followed by a clause explaining what it symbolizes or ensures | Delete the explanation if the next choice already carries it |
| Vague authority | “Everyone knew,” “experts believed,” or “it was said” supplies convenient credibility | Name the source, limit the knowledge, or keep it uncertain |
| Formulaic challenge section | A paragraph lists difficulties and ends with generic optimism | Choose one active obstacle and let the character encounter it |
| AI vocabulary clustering | Several abstract prestige words gather in one paragraph | Return to the character's work, objects, and current decision |
| Negative antithesis | Repeated “not X, but Y” manufactures depth | State the concrete Y, or let the contradiction remain in action |
| Forced triads | Descriptions, principles, and feelings repeatedly arrive in groups of three | Use the number the scene actually contains; a list may be one, two, four, or none |
| Synonym cycling | The protagonist becomes “the youth,” “the boy,” “the young warrior” to avoid repetition | Repeat the name or pronoun when it is clearest |
| False range | “From ancient gods to kitchen quarrels” pretends unrelated items form a scale | Name the actual scope or choose the relevant end |
| Quote bait | A paragraph bends toward a screenshot-ready maxim | Restore the listener, immediate stakes, and what happens after the line |
| Generic positive conclusion | “A new journey had begun” or “the future was full of possibility” | End on the specific next condition, choice, or cost |
| Formatting residue | Excess headings, bold labels, emoji, or chatbot preambles enter the manuscript | Deliver clean prose in the requested manuscript format |
| Over-qualification | Characters and narrators stack “perhaps, possibly, in some sense” without genuine uncertainty | Keep the exact uncertainty and remove defensive padding |
| Complete thematic cooperation | Weather, props, side characters, dialogue, and ending all confirm one theme | Let at least some elements pursue ordinary functions or competing meanings |

Do not mechanically remove all em dashes, complete sentences, lists, connective words, or abstract terms. Their repetition and function matter more than their presence.

General humanizer inventories are diagnostic references, not a drafting style. In fiction, “add personality,” “allow mess,” “vary rhythm,” and “be specific” must pass viewpoint, canon, relationship, and causality gates. Random digressions, fragments, slang, sharp opinions, two-item lists, or irregular punctuation can become a second machine formula.

## 4. Advanced scene-level mechanical symptoms

Use this inventory only after a draft shows a concrete problem. Choose one matching repair; never run the entire table as a writing checklist.

| Symptom | Evidence | Minimal repair |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning overdetermined | Most details simultaneously perform plot, theme, foreshadowing, and characterization | Return the weakest detail to ordinary use or remove one imposed meaning |
| Prop overfunctional | A newly introduced object exists only to test power, save the climax, or symbolize a relationship | Give it a credible use history, use an existing object, or remove it |
| Instant moral clarity | A character immediately diagnoses a system and chooses the mature response | Restore age, interest, fear, permission, delay, or a wrong first interpretation |
| Theme cooperation | Weather, strangers, teachers, institutions, and enemies all help state the same idea | Restore competing tasks, incomplete evidence, and ordinary functions |
| Subtext overlegible | Every pause and indirect line has one clean translation understood by everyone | Let attention differ and make one interpretation affect action without author confirmation |
| Competence gloss | Observation, inference, courage, wording, and morality are all impressive, including mistakes | Give the character a different-domain blind spot with real time, relationship, or resource cost |
| Aftermath compressed | Conflict ends and the cast resets after one reflective sentence | Carry one body, work, record, debt, cleanup, or relationship consequence forward |
| Ending thesis | The last sentence names the chapter's meaning or announces growth | Stop on the changed action, condition, or unfinished practical task |
| Fake withholding | Viewpoint recognizes something but narration delays the name for manufactured mystery | Name it when recognized; move suspense to the resulting choice or risk |
| Hook inflation | Revision adds attackers, weapons, revelations, or disasters unsupported by the plan | Find a consequential choice inside existing facts instead of increasing danger |
| Paragraph button | Ordinary paragraphs repeatedly end with a clever explanatory tag | Delete the tag; restore missing information earlier only if action cannot carry it |
| Uniform finish | Every paragraph is equally polished, witty, symbolic, or emotionally weighted | Choose the one place that needs precision and let transitions remain clear and ordinary |
| Scene function grid | Adjacent scenes separately “show power,” “deliver lore,” and “seed plot” but can swap freely | Carry an unfinished duty, body state, resource, relationship, or mistake causally between scenes |
| Didactic miniature | Lesson uses catalog questions and a precise fail–explain–instant-success demonstration | Keep the real class task and consequences; summarize routine teaching and leave some failure unresolved |
| Authority kit | Mentor gains credibility through abstract theme, triads, maxims, rhetorical questions, and a neat callback | Ground authority in work, tools, biased experience, limits, and specific past consequences |
| Atmosphere detached | Season or place is announced but never changes clothing, road, light, labor, time, or body | Select one environmental fact that alters the current task or cost |
| Triggerless interruption | A name, glance, sound, or sudden appearance automatically makes an active character stop, freeze, or lower a weapon | Decide whether the cue is strong enough for this person now; let the current motion finish or supply a concrete cognitive, relational, bodily, or tactical cause for interruption |
| Verb–body mismatch | A verb approximately suggests the intended result but the named object, material, or body would not naturally perform that action | Track the actual contact, force, sound source, or changed object and choose the ordinary verb for that physical event |

The symptom names are retrieval handles, not detector science. One occurrence may be intentional. Report only repeated evidence or a pivotal failure.

## 5. Scene-specific guidance

### Exposition

Attach information to a present decision. A rule becomes vivid when someone pays for misunderstanding it, exploits it, negotiates around it, repairs damage caused by it, or teaches only the part required for today's work.

If a lesson exists only to explain lore, first ask whether the characters would still attend it if the reader already knew the setting. If not, compress or relocate the information.

### Emotion

Use body reactions when the body genuinely interferes: breath affects speech, pain limits movement, nausea changes food, shaking spoils precision. Otherwise let emotion change attention, choice, timing, or social behavior.

Avoid instant moral clarity. A major event may first produce logistics, denial, a wrong conclusion, inappropriate humor, or an attempt to finish a small task.

### Action and combat

Track position, distance, available tools, intent, and failure consequence before naming spectacle. A powerful action should change terrain, resources, body, witnesses, or future options. Do not hide unclear causality behind light, speed, and impact adjectives.

A cue is not a command to stop. Hearing one's name, noticing a weapon, or seeing an expression may enter attention while the current step or strike continues. Interrupt motion only when the cue changes recognition, relationship stakes, balance, pain, or tactical choice enough to do so; then show where the unfinished motion and weapon go.

### War and institutional violence

Do not measure seriousness by corpse count alone. Show missing workers, altered routes, rationing, delayed messages, reused equipment, burial duties, frightened expertise, conflicting orders, and the gap between command maps and inhabited ground. Death matters through interrupted relationships and work as well as physical description.

### Comedy

Build comedy from stable needs and incompatible habits. A proud person chooses an undignified solution; a careful plan meets a petty material problem; two people protect different versions of the same relationship. Do not turn the cast temporarily stupid just to land a joke.

### Quiet scenes

A quiet scene can establish routine, dependency, class, attraction, distrust, recuperation, or the cost of earlier action. It does not need a hidden clue or cliffhanger. It needs a place in the story's lived continuity.

## 6. Rhythm without quotas

- Long sentences may carry careful thought, formal speech, labor, accumulation, or reluctance.
- Short sentences may carry urgency, certainty, shock, practiced coordination, or intimacy.
- Paragraphs group a continuous attention, action, or negotiation; do not insert a blank after every beat.
- Repetition may be character-owned emphasis, counting, ritual, self-soothing, or correction.
- Onomatopoeia and reduplication may belong to age, sound, relationship, or local speech; do not add them by quota.
- A chapter may end quietly. A serial needs forward desire, not identical cliffhangers.

## 6A. Delicacy without decorative overload

Do not treat "more detail" as longer description. A scene becomes finer when a small fact has an owner, a physical or social context, and a life beyond the adjective.

- Let sensation enter because it changes work, speech, route, comfort, error, or recognition.
- Let objects carry time through wear, repair, replacement, custody, and changed use.
- Let relationships alter allocation: who gets the dry place, who finishes the task, who notices but does not expose.
- Let emotion appear in timing, preparation, correction, avoidance, and habits that survive their original reason.
- Let one image be exact, then allow the next sentence to be plain.

Avoid sensory quotas, symbolic overload, decorative synonym chains, and equal polish across every sentence. Read `detail-and-texture.md` when a scene specifically needs richer texture or restrained beauty.

## 7. Original control example

Mechanical version:

> The ruined watchtower stood as a solemn testament to the devastating cost of war. It was not merely a building, but a symbol of everything the village had lost. Nevertheless, amid the challenges, hope endured.

Naturalized version:

> The watchtower had lost its stairs, so the night guard climbed by the outer stones. Halfway up, he passed the hook where his brother used to hang the lantern. The hook was still there. The lantern had been issued to somebody else.

The revision does not add randomness. It restores physical use, a specific relationship, institutional reuse, and an unstated emotional cost.

## 8. Final read

Read aloud once and ask:

1. Could any viewpoint character have noticed these same details in this order?
2. Do several paragraphs finish by explaining their own meaning?
3. Are multiple characters equally articulate about motives, systems, and themes?
4. Did revision make every sentence shorter, sharper, dirtier, or more casual in the same way?
5. Does the ending arise from an event or choice already present?

Fix only the evidence you can point to.
