# Drafting Engine: How to Put the Novel into Prose

Use this reference to draft, continue, rewrite, or polish fiction. Drafting converts selected story knowledge and a chosen narrative movement into reader experience through viewpoint or narrator, scene, summary, ellipsis, reflection, documents, action, syntax, dialogue, information timing, rhythm, and omission. It is not the stage for inventing an entire bible while sentences are being written. Read `character-scene-execution.md` when several prose layers must remain owned by distinct OCs rather than receiving separate sentence-level fixes.

## 1. Minimum drafting packet

Load only what can constrain the requested scene:

- story compass targets relevant now;
- current viewpoint character, identity invariant, experience residue, listener model, body, knowledge, relationships, voice, and OOC state;
- entry time, location, unfinished action, resources, and recent residue;
- world, system, faction, item, or bestiary facts that affect choices;
- dominant narrative engine, selected carrier, meaningful movement, changed reading or exit, and residue;
- one genre or prose target;
- author corrections and protected wording.

Do not load the whole bible and then mention everything retrieved. Missing knowledge may remain missing. Contradictions must be resolved or reported before prose polish.

## 2. Scene promise before the first sentence

Internally state:

```text
This scene lets the reader experience ___ as [viewpoint] tries to ___,
but ___ resists; the scene leaves ___ changed or unfinished.
```

This is not a visible template. It prevents atmosphere, dialogue, and lore from replacing movement.

A quiet scene may promise recuperation, routine, dependence, awkward repair, class difference, or a changed habit. It does not need a twist. A voice-, atmosphere-, or form-driven unit may instead promise an altered association, contradiction, absence, or reading.

## 2A. Choose the narrative carrier

Do not dramatize everything as a scene. Choose the carrier that lets the reader experience the relevant movement:

| Carrier | Use when | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Scene | interaction, uncertainty, bodily work, choice, or resistance must be experienced moment by moment | dialogue and gesture added to information that needs only summary |
| Summary | elapsed time, routine, repetition, travel, or low-variance process matters mainly through accumulation | abstract report that skips the one decisive variation |
| Ellipsis/transition | absence, discontinuity, or omitted duration creates useful pressure | hiding causally necessary information |
| Reflection/interiority | association, self-explanation, memory, or revaluation is the movement | complete author logic detached from present attention |
| Exposition | a rule or history must become usable now | encyclopedia pause or convenient lecture |
| Letter/document/record | authorship, audience, access, editing, or material survival changes meaning | ordinary narration placed under a date heading |
| Montage/recurrence | variation across repeated acts creates the pattern | list of examples with no changed association |

Several carriers may share one unit. Give each a job and a transition. Read `narrative-engines.md` when the carrier is driven by voice, atmosphere, idea, or form rather than conventional scene conflict.

## 3. Enter through action and attention

Begin where a current task, interruption, arrival, decision, failure, or need gives details a reason to appear. The first paragraph need not be dramatic; it must orient attention.

Useful movements:

```text
notice → assume → act → encounter mismatch → revise
do work → material friction → adjustment → social consequence
request → resistance → changed tactic → cost
```

Do not begin every chapter with weather, waking, a philosophical statement, a mysterious fragment, or a retrospective summary. Use them only when the viewpoint's present action makes them relevant.

## 4. Viewpoint and narration

Let the viewpoint determine:

- what is noticed first and ignored;
- names and categories used;
- confidence, bias, and mistaken interpretation;
- distance from body and emotion;
- which background facts become relevant;
- sentence movement and degree of reflection.

A hungry person may notice portions; a quartermaster notices spoilage and missing seals; a child notices punishment and who is watching. The narrator may be more articulate than the character without gaining forbidden knowledge.

Do not delay the name of something the viewpoint recognizes solely to manufacture mystery.

## 5. Description and world information

Describe through use, obstruction, comparison, labor, status, memory, or consequence. Select details rather than scanning evenly.

World information enters when it changes:

- what someone attempts;
- price, time, route, permission, or risk;
- how an object is used or repaired;
- who may speak, enter, own, study, travel, or refuse;
- what a character believes and later corrects.

Routine explanation may be summarized. Quote instruction, doctrine, and history only when the wording itself affects relationship, authority, misunderstanding, or action.

Avoid student-question relays, convenient newcomer lectures, catalog dialogue, and fail–explain–instant-success tutorials.

## 5A. Fine detail and texture

Detail is not coverage. Select the detail made visible by the viewpoint's present task, body, need, knowledge, avoidance, or relationship. When a passage feels thin, try one short movement:

```text
notice → use or contact → resistance/change → adjustment or consequence
```

Useful texture may be operational (how a thing is handled), relational (who receives, repairs, hides, or yields), temporal (wear, replacement, residue, changed routine), or sensory (a physical fact that alters action). Use only what the beat supports. An object does not need to perform every function, and every sentence does not need a memorable image.

Let one precise sentence stand beside plain connective prose. Figurative language should come from the viewpoint's lived vocabulary and stop before it explains itself. Read `detail-and-texture.md` for examples, scene calibration, and a light revision pass.

## 6. Dialogue as character action

For each key speaker, retrieve two anchors and one live model:

- vocabulary source;
- permission with this listener.
- what the speaker believes this listener knows, will accept or misread, and must not conclude.

Then ask what the speaker wants heard first: answer, request, refusal, warning, complaint, joke, reassurance, concealment, or silence.

Let sentence length follow purpose and identity. Emergency cooperation, professional reports, and simple facts may be direct. Caregivers, officials, teachers, traders, and formal speakers may use complete long sentences. Colloquial speech is not compulsory fragmentation.

Responses follow what listeners care about and can acknowledge. Direct answers, partial answers, misunderstanding, postponement, task continuation, or nonresponse may all be natural.

Use action tags for speaker clarity, bodily interference, parallel work, or a changed situation—not as emotion decoration after every line.

Read `dialogue.md` for detailed symptom repair. Repair at most one recurring dialogue pattern per pass unless the author requests a workshop.

## 7. Emotion and interiority

Let emotion change attention, timing, price, route, speech, work, risk, appetite, sleep, routine, or relationship behavior.

Do not require instant self-knowledge. A person may first handle logistics, deny, joke, become overly polite, finish a small task, misidentify the cause, or react much later.

Body detail belongs when the body interferes: breath changes speech, pain changes movement, nausea changes food, shaking changes precision. Do not rotate through heartbeat, dry throat, clenched fists, and widened eyes as an emotion checklist.

Interior thought may be intelligent and complete when the viewpoint supports it. The risk is universal author logic: every character accurately explains motive, system, theme, and best action at the convenient moment.

## 8. Action, combat, and war

Track:

- intent and target;
- position, distance, route, visibility, and timing;
- body, equipment, ammunition, magic input, and failure consequence;
- opponent knowledge and adaptation;
- witnesses, terrain change, resource use, and exit options.

Spectacle follows causality. A powerful action should change bodies, ground, tools, witnesses, resources, law, or later options.

War seriousness also lives in transport, rationing, missing labor, medical triage, burial, records, delayed messages, desertion, refugees, equipment reuse, civilian bargains, and veterans after victory.

## 9. Paragraph, rhythm, transition, and unit ending

Let a paragraph hold while the same attention, action, negotiation, or reflection continues. Break it when the reader's footing genuinely changes; do not impose sentence counts or add a blank after every beat.

- Long sentences may carry accumulation, careful thought, procedure, bargaining, reluctance, or formal speech.
- Short sentences may carry urgency, certainty, practiced coordination, shock, intimacy, or refusal.
- Repetition may show counting, ritual, correction, insistence, self-soothing, or sound.
- Ordinary transitions may remain plain.

Between scenes, carry at least one real continuity when relevant: unfinished duty, time, body, resource, relationship pressure, mistake, promise, weather, or route. Do not use every transition to introduce new lore.

End on a changed condition, choice, refusal, arrival, unfinished task, practical consequence, altered association, formal reorientation, or quiet continuation. Serial momentum comes from forward desire and residue, not identical cliffhangers or thesis lines. A standalone or fragment does not owe the reader a serial hook.

## 10. Genre-sensitive prose calibration

Use reference works differently for prose than for ideation. Structure may be studied from summaries and broad comparison; sentence-level calibration requires lawful primary text, licensed previews, author-published web text, or a user-provided sample.

| Drafting target | Comparative cluster | Transferable prose question |
|---|---|---|
| Progression clarity | 《斗破苍穹》《武动乾坤》《凡人修仙传》 | Is lack, effort, resource, proof, and reversal readable in the present scene? |
| Close isekai growth | 《无职转生》《Re:从零开始的异世界生活》《本好きの下剋上》 | Does body, class, work, failure, and old self-deception filter what enters prose? |
| Practical institutions | 《记录的地平线》《关于我转生变成史莱姆这档事》《十二国记》 | Does administration appear through decisions, work, bargaining, delay, and unequal consequences rather than minutes? |
| Relationship dialogue | 《狼与香辛料》《我的青春恋爱物语果然有问题》《冰菓》《龙与虎》 | Do lines simultaneously reveal information choice, permission, self-protection, and ordinary shared life without universal wit? |
| War viewpoint | 《86—不存在的战区—》《银河英雄传说》《幼女战记》 | What is visible from body, command, bureaucracy, distance, and civilian work, and what remains inaccessible? |
| Occult and mystery | 《诡秘之主》《来自新世界》《冰菓》 | Does evidence precede explanation and alter action under uncertainty? |
| Contemporary, literary, and quiet fiction | project manuscript plus lawful samples selected for the exact language/form | Does attention, routine, relation, place, voice, or recurrence produce a changed reading without manufactured escalation? |
| Documentary or experimental form | project form contract plus lawful samples of the relevant medium | Does source, audience, access, omission, and placement change meaning rather than merely decorate narration? |

Convert the answer into an anonymous target. Do not reproduce syntax, signature metaphors, catchphrases, scene patterns, or a living author's distinctive style.

## 11. Anti-AI drafting model

AI-mechanical prose often completes more logic, meaning, symmetry, instruction, and closure than the scene or people require. The positive alternative is not random disorder. Give prose an owner and cost:

```text
specific viewpoint + uneven knowledge + relationship permission
+ material/social resistance + causal choice + persistent aftermath
```

During drafting, choose at most one likely risk. After drafting, scan for observable repetition. When the author explicitly reports AI or mechanical writing, read `anti-ai-execution.md` first: locate the deepest failed layer, route one repair, and stop after regression passes. Read `natural-prose.md` only for the matched prose symptom and `dialogue.md` only for sustained speech failure. Do not use AI-detector scores as literary evidence or run a full NG checklist sentence by sentence.

## 12. Three-source repair hierarchy

NW combines three useful source traditions without carrying their full runtime:

1. **WW** — long-form scene NG, dialogue ownership, author feedback, chapter planning, and genre-specific craft.
2. **Humanizer-zh** — inflated significance, promotional evaluation, vague attribution, formulaic triads, negative antithesis, synonym cycling, false range, formatting residue, over-qualification, and generic conclusion.
3. **InkOS** — separate intent, canon, current focus, plans, draft, audit, revision, and settlement; retrieve task-scoped context and keep forecasts non-canonical.

Apply them in this order:

```text
truth and intent → character and scene causality → prose/dialogue evidence
→ smallest repair → reread → accepted-state settlement
```

Do not use a general language symptom to override a character's valid complete sentence, formal register, repetition, or abstraction.

## 13. Revision passes

### Pass 1: truth

Check author intent, canon status, event sequence, character knowledge, body, resources, location, system rules, faction action, item custody, and species conditions.

### Pass 2: character and scene

Check OOC, dominant engine, selected carrier, meaningful movement, changed exit or reading, and residue. For action-causal scenes, also check want, resistance, attempt, and choice or failure. Repair structure, movement, or carrier before adjectives.

### Pass 3: expression

Choose at most three high-impact categories and one dialogue category. Repair local evidence; preserve liked wording and valid roughness.

### Pass 4: read and regression

Read the whole exchange or surrounding scene. Check clarity, voice differences, paragraph movement, information placement, and whether revisions made everything equally short, witty, gritty, emotional, or polished.

### Pass 5: settlement

Only after author acceptance, update the affected story-memory domains. Drafted or proposed events do not become canon automatically.

## 14. Drafting tests

1. **Ownership**: could another viewpoint notice the same details in the same order?
2. **OOC**: does decisive action, silence, attention, or interpretation arise from current state and pressure?
3. **Speech**: does each line belong to this speaker with this listener?
4. **Information**: does exposition alter present action or cost?
5. **Resistance**: does the world answer attempts rather than explain itself conveniently?
6. **Aftermath**: what condition, permission, association, implication, or formal relation persists beyond the paragraph or unit?
7. **Rhythm**: did revision impose one sentence, paragraph, or ending pattern?
8. **Clarity**: can necessary objects, subjects, positions, and causes be recovered?
9. **AI-mechanical symptom**: is there repeated evidence, not merely a disliked word?
10. **Source boundary**: are reference names and surface imitation absent from the prose brief?
11. **Detail**: do the most precise details arise from present attention and change use, feeling, knowledge, association, or consequence rather than merely increasing density?
12. **Carrier**: was the material dramatized, summarized, omitted, reflected, or documented for a reason, or did one mode swallow the whole work?

Fix only the tests relevant to the request. Deliver clean prose, not the internal checklist.
