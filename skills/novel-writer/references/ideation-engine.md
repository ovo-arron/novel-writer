# Ideation Engine: How to Conceive a Novel

Use this reference when inventing, expanding, comparing, or repairing a premise, setting, character, faction, system, event chain, arc, volume, chapter, mystery, or future direction. Ideation is causal reasoning under author intent, not unrestricted trope generation.

## 1. Ideation output

Match the artifact to the request:

- premise engine;
- story compass;
- one or more bible entries;
- conflict or event chain;
- character or relationship design;
- system, faction, item, species, or location expansion;
- story, volume, chapter, or scene outline;
- mystery evidence structure;
- labeled alternate branches;
- diagnosis of a broken idea.

Do not answer every ideation request with a full story bible. State assumptions and keep alternatives non-canonical.

## 2. Start from pressure, not inventory

Begin with a condition that forces recurring choices:

```text
person or group wants X
under rule or condition Y
but action Z protects one value while endangering another
```

Weak inventory:

> A reincarnated demon attends an academy, learns seven elements, joins a guild, obtains a sacred sword, and fights a hero.

Stronger pressure engine:

> A weak demon child earns safety by becoming useful to a border state preparing for renewed human war. Every skill that wins trust also makes refusal of military service harder, while former classmates grow up inside the opposing civilization.

The second idea can generate family, education, labor, class, identity, state, friendship, and war choices before any power catalog exists.

## 3. The causal expansion loop

Use this loop on a new element:

```text
difference → ordinary use → constraint or cost → beneficiary
→ cost bearer → adaptation or resistance → immediate event
→ second-order consequence → persistent residue
```

Example: “magic can preserve food” is not yet a world.

- Ordinary use: cities store seasonal grain and fish.
- Constraint: preservation consumes a mined salt controlled by temples.
- Beneficiary: armies and merchants travel farther.
- Cost bearer: mining villages lose labor and water.
- Adaptation: counterfeit salt and temple inspections appear.
- Event: a border garrison receives inert stock.
- Second-order consequence: commanders accuse traders; traders blame temple monopoly.
- Residue: food policy becomes a legitimacy crisis, not merely a failed spell.

Stop when the requested scale is supported. Do not expand every idea to world history.

## 4. Character-driven reasoning

For each decisive beat, reason from:

1. current want;
2. desired image with the present listener or group;
3. feared confirmation and the value or relationship under threat;
4. knowledge and false belief;
5. competence and blind spot;
6. body, resources, status, and available alternatives;
7. habitual strategy and face-saving method;
8. cost the character is willing or unwilling to pay.

Then ask: what would this person try before the plot's preferred choice, and what trouble could that reasonable attempt cause? Opposition should answer the attempt for its own reason. If the event survives after removing the desired image, relationship permission, and habitual strategy, it is not character-generated yet.

A planned arc is credible when later choices remember earlier costs. Do not design a “growth moment” first and reverse-engineer a speech to justify it.

## 5. Opposition and independent futures

For every durable source of resistance, define:

- the future it wants;
- why that future appears necessary from inside its knowledge;
- constituency, resources, operations, and limits;
- acceptable losses and red lines;
- internal disagreement;
- what it does if the protagonist never appears.

Conflict becomes renewable when both sides can act. Avoid antagonists whose strategy is a sequence of challenges calibrated to protagonist growth.

## 6. Second-order consequence

After any major invention or event, ask selectively:

- What becomes cheaper, rarer, safer, criminal, prestigious, obsolete, or politically necessary?
- Who changes occupation, route, marriage, education, migration, or inheritance strategy?
- Which institution gains records, jurisdiction, or monopoly?
- What maintenance, waste, dependency, black market, superstition, or inequality appears?
- Which relationship changes because someone now owes, fears, needs, or knows more?
- What solution creates the next problem?

Choose two or three consequences that affect the story. More consequences are not automatically more realistic.

## 7. Scale and long-form renewal

Escalate by widening consequence, not only power:

```text
body/household → peers/work → institution/faction
→ settlement/region → state/war → world order
```

Move outward when earlier relationships, skills, resources, and mistakes create leverage at the next scale. Preserve lower-scale obligations after expansion.

For each arc or volume, define:

- practical objective;
- relationship under pressure;
- one world rule tested in more than one context;
- midpoint condition that invalidates the easiest plan;
- climax choice;
- residue that becomes future pressure;
- question settled and question transformed.

Do not merely introduce a stronger enemy when the current arc resolves.

## 8. Information, mystery, and revelation

Separate:

```text
reality → evidence → access → belief → decision → later reinterpretation
```

Design evidence that changes action before certainty. Let witnesses, records, bodies, items, institutions, and environments carry different reliability and incentives.

A revelation is earned when earlier evidence becomes newly legible without requiring characters to ignore the obvious. Do not withhold a fact from a viewpoint that already knows it.

## 9. Branch generation and comparison

Generate branches from different decisive choices, not cosmetic endings.

For each branch, record:

- divergence choice and who makes it;
- next three to five causal beats;
- character growth or regression pressure;
- world, faction, location, item, or ecological consequences;
- reader-promise fit;
- opportunity gained;
- sacrifice and continuity risk;
- whether the branch closes or renews the long engine.

Keep branches isolated. Selecting one updates the plan; only accepted manuscript or explicit author confirmation updates canon.

## 10. Reference triangulation

Use reference works to answer different questions rather than blend their surfaces.

Example clusters:

| Ideation need | Comparative works | Extract, then anonymize |
|---|---|---|
| Visible progression and serial momentum | 《斗破苍穹》《武动乾坤》《凡人修仙传》《大主宰》 | lack, resource, proof, obligation, and widening opposition |
| Reincarnation as development | 《无职转生》《Re:从零开始的异世界生活》《十二国记》《本好きの下剋上》 | body, learning, old flaws, failure, class, responsibility, and delayed mastery |
| Institutions and state-building | 《关于我转生变成史莱姆这档事》《记录的地平线》《OVERLORD》《十二国记》 | protection, administration, misinterpretation, legitimacy, and unequal consequence |
| War and politics | 《86—不存在的战区—》《银河英雄传说》《幼女战记》《哥布林杀手》 | logistics, political alternatives, bureaucracy, local expertise, and civilian cost |
| Mystery and hidden systems | 《诡秘之主》《来自新世界》《冰菓》 | evidence access, layered belief, ordinary traces, and institutional truth |
| Relationship-driven conflict | 《我的青春恋爱物语果然有问题》《狼与香辛料》《龙与虎》《青春猪头少年》系列 | self-protective interpretation, bargaining, domestic consequence, restrained concern |

Triangulation procedure:

1. choose at most one mechanism from each relevant work;
2. state which project problem it addresses;
3. state incompatibilities among mechanisms;
4. remove names, signature powers, character configurations, famous scenes, catchphrases, and plot sequences;
5. generate original people, pressures, objects, places, and outcomes;
6. run the source-reconstruction test: if the source scene is easy to infer, abstract further.

Plot summaries support structural hypotheses only. Sentence-level conclusions require lawful primary text or a user-provided sample.

## 11. Genre-specific ideation lenses

### Cultivation and progression

Ask what is lacking now, what resource or practice changes options, how growth is verified, and what duty or stronger opposition follows. Preserve weaknesses outside the progression axis.

### Isekai and light-novel fantasy

Ask what the body, language, family, class, law, work, and local knowledge prevent the protagonist from applying. Previous knowledge should meet material and social resistance.

### School and youth

Ask which rules, invitations, seating, records, clubs, families, messages, reputations, and small public choices change relationships. Intelligence needs a blind spot.

### Serious fantasy and war

Ask what maps, orders, supply tables, and victory reports omit; who carries, repairs, buries, treats, waits, flees, records, and remains necessary afterward.

### Mystery and occult

Ask what evidence exists before explanation, who can access it, what mistaken model remains reasonable, and what action changes under uncertainty.

## 12. Ideation tests

- **Why-now test**: what condition makes this event happen now rather than months earlier?
- **Deletion test**: what later action, cost, knowledge, or relationship becomes impossible if removed?
- **Swap test**: can another character, faction, place, or item perform the same function unchanged?
- **Resistance test**: does the world or opposition answer the protagonist's first attempt?
- **Independent-future test**: what do other actors do without the protagonist?
- **Second-order test**: what adapts after the initial effect?
- **OOC gate**: does the decisive action belong to the character under current pressure?
- **Canon gate**: is this fact, belief, plan, branch, or uncertainty?
- **Reader-promise test**: does this direction renew the book's attraction?
- **Reference-removal test**: does the idea remain specific and useful after source names vanish?

Use the tests that match the risk. Do not display all of them in the author's artifact.

## 13. AI-mechanical ideation failures

- Trope inventory substitutes for a renewable pressure.
- Every category receives one character, faction, item, species, and future payoff.
- Symmetry becomes so complete that people act as diagram labels.
- Every detail is useful, every ordinary object foreshadows, and every scene proves something.
- The protagonist is the hidden cause or solution of all institutions and histories.
- Opposition escalates in exact training order.
- One failure teaches precisely the lesson needed for immediate success.
- A twist changes facts but not anyone's next choice.
- Complexity is created by proper nouns rather than interacting incentives.
- All moral uncertainty resolves into the protagonist's correct analysis.
- Alternate branches differ only in enemy, location, or spectacle.
- Reference works are mixed as a list of recognizable surface features.
- Character traits are assigned to scenes, but no event grows from a self-protective strategy meeting independent resistance.
- Side characters possess biographies yet have no task, clock, refusal, or next action outside the protagonist's need.

Repair pressure, agency, causal carryover, and unresolved cost before adding more lore.
