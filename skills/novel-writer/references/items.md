# Items and Artifacts Bible

Use this reference for ordinary possessions, tools, documents, weapons, medicine, currency, relics, magical artifacts, vehicles, clothing, buildings-as-assets, and any object whose function, ownership, condition, or history matters.

## 1. Object before symbol

Let an item first exist through use: carried, worn, eaten, repaired, stored, borrowed, broken, cleaned, traded, lost, copied, or ignored. Meaning may accumulate through ownership and events. Do not require every object to symbolize a relationship, foreshadow a climax, and solve a plot problem.

Classify narrative importance only when helpful:

- background object;
- recurring practical object;
- relationship object;
- evidence or information carrier;
- scarce strategic asset;
- signature tool;
- artifact or world-changing object.

Background objects do not need full records.

Distinguish owner from bearer. A stolen crown, borrowed knife, issued rifle, inherited debt note, and sealed letter create different rights and risks.

## 3. Function, limit, and failure

For an important tool or magical item, ask:

- What exact action can it perform?
- What input, skill, permission, body, time, or environment does it require?
- How does a user know it worked?
- How does it fail, degrade, misfire, or produce side effects?
- Who can repair, authenticate, copy, disable, or counterfeit it?
- What cheaper substitute exists?
- What problem remains unsolved after successful use?

Do not balance every item as a game statistic. Limits should arise from the world and produce decisions.

## 4. Custody and knowledge

Track separately:

- physical custody;
- legal ownership;
- social claim;
- access or key;
- knowledge of location;
- knowledge of true function;
- ability to use it;
- evidence linking it to an event.

An item can drive plot through transfer, concealment, authentication, damage, repair, inheritance, confiscation, duplication, or refusal to surrender—not only through activation.

## 5. Artifacts and powerful items

A credible artifact has a place in history and institutions:

1. Why was it made or discovered?
2. Why has it not already solved the world's obvious problems?
3. Who has tried to control, regulate, worship, destroy, copy, or hide it?
4. What knowledge about it is wrong?
5. What does possession expose the bearer to?
6. What ordinary labor keeps it usable?
7. What political, economic, military, or religious arrangement changes if custody changes?

Powerful does not mean universally useful. An artifact may be decisive in one environment and cumbersome, illegal, expensive, or misunderstood elsewhere.

## 6. Economy, law, and craft

For items that matter repeatedly, connect:

```text
materials → maker and skill → production time → distribution
→ price or allocation → maintenance → repair, reuse, disposal, or inheritance
```

Ask who is allowed to own or carry it, what records follow it, and whether scarcity comes from material, labor, law, monopoly, transport, or knowledge.

Craft detail should change action or credibility. Do not invent miniature standard test materials solely so the protagonist can demonstrate a power; use an existing tool, a real repair, a complete object someone needs, or a genuine production process.

## 7. Item continuity

Before drafting with a recurring item, retrieve:

- last confirmed location and custodian;
- current condition and remaining quantity;
- who knows about it;
- obligations or danger attached to it;
- whether it has already been used, revealed, promised, spent, copied, or destroyed.

After the scene, update only actual changes. A planned handoff is not a completed transfer. An item mentioned in a discarded draft remains where canon last placed it.

## 8. Common failures

- Inventory appears only when a solution is needed.
- A common purchase receives a ceremonial group reveal.
- Every old object is secretly important.
- An artifact's limits change to fit the scene.
- Characters know an item's function without access or evidence.
- Ownership, custody, and location are treated as the same fact.
- Weapons have spectacle but no weight, range, maintenance, training, law, or ammunition.
- Medicines cure without dosage, availability, recovery time, or triage choices.
- Documents reveal truth without author, chain of custody, forgery risk, or reader access.
- A renamed ordinary object becomes pseudo-specialized lore.

Repair use, custody, and consequence before adding another magical property.
