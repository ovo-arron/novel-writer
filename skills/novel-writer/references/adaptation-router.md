# Universal Fiction Adaptation Router

Use this reference before importing genre habits, serial pacing, speculative systems, or platform conventions into a project. The router changes which NW tools are active; it does not replace the three cores.

## 1. Adaptation principle

Start from the author's intended reading experience and the project's evidence, not from NW's strongest existing examples. A form or genre changes pressure, evidence, pacing, and reader expectation; it does not supply a mandatory beat sheet.

Use this order:

```text
author intent and existing text → length/release mode → narrative form
→ dominant narrative engine → genre promises → realism/research constraints
→ language/cultural register → active memory domains → task packet
```

Keep the profile compact. Record only decisions that alter conception, drafting, retrieval, or review. If the project has an accepted profile, preserve it until the author or manuscript changes it.

## 2. Build the minimum profile

Capture these fields internally or in Core III's `adaptation-profile.md`:

| Field | Useful choices | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Work form | short story, novella, standalone novel, cycle, trilogy/series, serial | structural horizon, summary ladder, settlement unit |
| Release mode | complete manuscript, periodic serial, episodic, interactive, unknown | hook cadence, recap tolerance, revision freedom |
| Narrative form | first/third/omniscient, single/multi-POV, linear/nonlinear, epistolary/documentary | knowledge ownership, transitions, source handling |
| Story engine | action, character strategy, relationship, investigation, voice/consciousness, atmosphere/place, idea/institution, form/document, progression | what renews meaningful movement |
| Genre promise | one primary and zero to two secondary promises | reader questions and acceptable resolutions |
| Reality relation | realist, historical, speculative, alternate history, surreal, allegorical | canon and research boundaries |
| Language/register | target language, region, era, social register, translation posture | idiom, address, exposition, dialogue permissions |
| Audience/boundary | age range, content boundary, platform constraint if explicit | scene treatment, not artistic value |
| Research risk | low, factual, sensitive, high-stakes | verification and uncertainty handling |
| Active domains | required, conditional, inactive | retrieval and memory footprint |

Do not manufacture missing choices. An unknown platform stays unknown; a mixed genre may retain an unresolved priority until a specific scene forces the decision.

## 3. Route by length and release mode

### Short story

Use one dominant engine and a small number of meaningful movements. Each included unit should alter action, relation, interpretation, association, implication, or formal arrangement. Do not shrink a novel outline mechanically; design around concentration, omission, echo, and one earned reorientation.

Core III can remain minimal: source map, adaptation profile, character evidence, working state, and an accepted-unit entry may be enough. Volumes, rolling chapter windows, and million-character indexing are inactive.

### Novella

Allow one central arc plus a limited counter-current. Compress travel, institutions, and backstory unless their experienced friction is the story. Use parts or sequences only when they mark genuine condition changes.

### Standalone novel

Use whole-work inheritance: opening conditions create middle choices, middle consequences make the ending possible, and the ending settles or deliberately transforms the central promise. Chapters may be long, short, titled, or absent. Do not force serial micro-hooks; preserve cumulative tension and local scene exits.

### Series or cycle

Give each volume or installment a local settlement and a changed inheritance. Preserve continuing obligations without making every ending a cliffhanger. Track what can recur, what must evolve, and what has lost pressure.

### Web serial or episodic release

Activate `serial-production.md`, rolling horizons, accepted-unit settlement, and reader-memory support. Hooks are changes in expectation, danger, desire, information, or obligation—not compulsory shocks at every chapter end. Platform-specific word counts apply only when the author supplies or requests them.

## 4. Route by narrative form

| Form | Primary control | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Close first person | owned attention, self-protective interpretation, missing knowledge | narrator becomes authorial analyst |
| Close third person | viewpoint distance and sensory/conceptual vocabulary | neutral camera plus inserted thoughts |
| Omniscient | deliberate authority, selection, transitions, social range | accidental head-hopping |
| Multi-POV | each viewpoint owns information, stakes, diction, and independent future | same voice wearing different names |
| Nonlinear | causal and emotional legibility despite chronology changes | chronology puzzle without consequence |
| Epistolary/documentary | document purpose, author, audience, access, alteration, absence | every document sounds like exposition |
| Collective or experimental | stable rule of attention and controlled exceptions | novelty without readable pressure |

Record story chronology separately from reveal order. In documentary forms, track who produced, received, edited, found, or suppressed each artifact.

Read `narrative-engines.md` when conventional conflict and transformation do not explain why the work continues. Voice-driven, atmosphere-driven, form-driven, slice-of-life, steadfast, static, tragic, and experimental fiction still require meaningful recurrence or reorientation, but not compulsory escalation, an antagonist, or a positive character arc.

## 5. Route by story problem

Use genre as a promise-and-pressure lens. Select only the rows that matter.

| Story problem | Keep active | Do not assume |
|---|---|---|
| Contemporary/realist | work, money, family, status, body, institutions, place, relationship permissions | speculative systems or neatly symbolic events |
| Literary/character-driven | attention, contradiction, memory, moral pressure, image recurrence, form | plotlessness, obscurity, or uniformly elevated prose |
| Romance | directional desire, consent, vulnerability, compatibility, external life, changed intimacy | fixed beat sheet, guaranteed pairing, conflict by avoidable stupidity |
| Mystery/crime | evidence, access, opportunity, belief, concealment, investigative cost, fair reveal | omniscient clues, genius monologues, twist as substitute for causality |
| Thriller | narrowing time/options, pursuit, operational competence, reversals with cost | continuous loudness or invulnerable protagonists |
| Horror | vulnerability, uncertainty, boundary violation, anticipation, residue | monster explanation, gore quota, or total helplessness |
| Historical | period institutions, material life, language distance, source uncertainty, plausible agency | modern attitudes in costume or invented facts presented as verified |
| Science fiction | altered premise, operational consequences, limits, institutions, human adaptation | encyclopedia exposition or technical certainty without evidence |
| Fantasy/speculative | rules only where action needs them, culture, power distribution, consequence | magic taxonomy, maps, species, or quests by default |
| Progression/cultivation | visible lack, costly method, verified change, changing opposition, larger obligation | endless rank labels, effortless mastery, repetitive humiliation/reward |
| War/political | logistics, command limits, civilian life, legitimacy, propaganda, aftermath | maps as experience, balanced sides, or suffering as decoration |
| Comedy/satire | stable desires and flaws, social target, timing, consequence | random quips, universal stupidity, or jokes erasing stakes |
| Family/workplace/social | role obligations, private history, unequal dependence, routine, reputation | every conflict becoming a speech about the theme |
| Adventure/quest | route, resources, local objectives, companions, changing map of risk | interchangeable encounters or travel montage as progress |

Genre blends require a hierarchy. The primary promise decides what must be paid off; secondary genres change the route and texture. If two promises conflict, state the tradeoff for the current sequence rather than averaging them.

## 6. Route by realism and research risk

Separate four categories:

1. verified external fact;
2. plausible but unverified working assumption;
3. deliberate fictional alteration;
4. character belief, error, rumor, or propaganda.

Historical, medical, legal, scientific, political, cultural, and other high-stakes claims require appropriate research when accuracy matters. Cite research in planning or notes when requested; do not turn manuscript prose into a report. Preserve uncertainty when sources disagree.

For alternate history or speculative realism, record the divergence and propagate consequences through institutions, labor, technology, language, and ordinary life. A fictional exception does not license unrelated inaccuracies.

## 7. Route by language and culture

Write idiomatically in the target language and register. Record:

- target language and regional/period register;
- names, honorifics, kinship terms, address levels, and code-switching rules;
- what the viewpoint can naturally name or explain;
- whether translation-like syntax is desired, tolerated, or prohibited;
- culturally specific assumptions that need research rather than substitution.

Do not translate idioms word for word, simulate Japanese light-novel Chinese by default, or flatten every culture into contemporary internet speech. A multilingual character may misunderstand, translate internally, switch registers, or avoid a term; use these as character actions, not decorative foreignness.

## 8. Activate memory domains selectively

Classify each domain as `required`, `conditional`, or `inactive` for the current work.

- Characters and events are usually required, but their depth follows the form.
- Worldbuilding includes ordinary social reality; it is not synonymous with invented lore.
- Systems activate when repeatable rules constrain choices, including law, bureaucracy, medicine, technology, ritual, or magic.
- Factions activate when organizations act independently.
- Locations activate when access, distance, layout, ownership, or memory matters.
- Items activate when custody, use, scarcity, evidence, inheritance, or condition matters.
- Living world activates when ecology, animals, plants, disease, nonhuman beings, or anomalies alter action.

Inactive means “do not retrieve or fill by default,” not “forbidden forever.” Promote a domain when the manuscript makes it consequential.

## 9. Mixed genres and changing modes

When a project changes mode across parts or viewpoints, keep one project-level profile plus a scoped override:

```text
project promise → current part/viewpoint override → scene task
```

An investigative middle can serve a romance; a comic viewpoint can exist inside serious war; a literary standalone can contain speculative rules. The override changes local pacing and evidence handling without erasing the whole-work promise.

After a deliberate mode change is accepted, record its scope and transition evidence in Core III. Do not reinterpret earlier chapters retroactively unless the author asks for revision.

## 10. Adaptation checks

Before conception or drafting, ask only what affects the task:

- Is serial momentum active, or did NW import it from habit?
- Is conflict, escalation, or character transformation truly the engine, or did NW import it from habit?
- Is a genre beat required by the project's promise, or merely familiar?
- Does this form need chapters, volumes, systems, factions, or bestiary entries?
- Does the chosen viewpoint have access to this fact and this vocabulary?
- Is a factual claim verified, intentionally altered, uncertain, or held only by a character?
- Does the target language sound native to its chosen register?
- Is the current output proportionate to the work's length and the user's request?

The successful route is the smallest one that preserves the work's own pressure, form, language, and evidence.
