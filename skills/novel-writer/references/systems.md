## 1. System versus isolated effect

A system is a repeatable relationship among inputs, actions, constraints, and outcomes. A one-time miracle, rumor, or unexplained anomaly may remain an event or bestiary entry.

Worldbuilding stores how systems reshape society. This domain stores the operational rule, knowledge levels, interfaces, limits, exceptions, and terminology.

## 2. System record

```text
Name, aliases, and who uses each term:
Status and source:
Domain: magic / cultivation / technology / medicine / language / calendar / rank / other
Observable capability:
Inputs and prerequisites:
Operation or process:
Outputs and verification:
Limits, costs, failure modes, and uncertainty:
Training, certification, inheritance, access, and exclusion:
Maintenance, recovery, repair, or replenishment:
Known counters, substitutes, and interactions:
Formal theory, practical knowledge, popular belief, and propaganda:
Economic, legal, military, religious, and household consequences:
Exceptions and disputed cases:
Linked characters, factions, events, items, species, and locations:
Open uncertainty:
```

Do not force a mysterious system to reveal rules the manuscript has not established.

## 3. Operational rule

Write the smallest form that predicts action:

```text
Under condition X, agent Y can attempt action Z by spending or risking C;
success changes A, failure changes B, and observers can verify it through E.
```

Rules need not be numerical. They must be consistent enough that characters can form expectations and readers can understand consequential choices.

## 4. Knowledge and training

Separate:

- objective behavior established by events;
- formal theory;
- professional craft knowledge;
- household practice;
- religious doctrine;
- rumor and superstition;
- individual mistaken model.

Track who can teach, certify, test, repair, research, copy, suppress, or monopolize knowledge. Training may require body development, materials, permission, language, time, trust, and repeated failure.

Do not turn training into a tutorial scene by default. Competence may emerge through work, partial instruction, practice across time, and consequences that remain unresolved.

## 5. Social and material consequences

For a powerful system, test only relevant second-order effects:

- labor and professions;
- class and access;
- law, records, and enforcement;
- warfare, policing, and logistics;
- medicine, disability, lifespan, and care;
- transport and communication;
- food, manufacturing, repair, and waste;
- religion, ritual, legitimacy, and taboo;
- education, family strategy, migration, and inheritance.

If teleportation, healing, communication, or mass production is common, explain the boundaries that keep ordinary institutions recognizable—or change those institutions.

## 6. Progression and power

Track progression through changed options, not numbers alone:

- what the character can attempt now;
- what cost becomes bearable or newly visible;
- what proof others accept;
- what duty, attention, legal status, or stronger opposition follows;
- what earlier weakness remains outside the progression axis.

Do not let power growth automatically improve judgment, social skill, morality, recovery, money, or political legitimacy.

## 7. Terminology and naming

A term needs at least one source:

- canon already uses it;
- a community or profession repeatedly needs the distinction;
- law, ritual, education, trade, or technology standardizes it;
- the current action cannot remain clear without naming it.

Record aliases by group. A scholar, soldier, child, village healer, and official record may name the same effect differently.

Avoid pseudo-specialized compound names created only to make the world sound complete. Use plain local function until a distinction becomes operational.

## 8. Exceptions and change

For each exception, determine whether it is:

- an established edge case;
- a hidden variable;
- a mistaken theory;
- a rare item, body, place, species, or event interaction;
- deliberate rule-breaking with cost;
- a proposed future reveal.

An exception should deepen the model or expose a false model, not erase limits whenever the protagonist needs success.

When a system changes, trace who notices first, who denies it, which records become unreliable, and what earlier plans fail.

## 9. System audit

1. Can the rule predict a meaningful action without explaining the entire cosmology?
2. Are cost and failure experienced rather than appended as flavor?
3. Who benefits, who is excluded, and who maintains the system?
4. Does society reflect common capabilities?
5. Do characters possess only the knowledge they could acquire?
6. Are progression proofs and ranks socially meaningful?
7. Do items, species, locations, and other systems interact consistently?
8. Are terms used by actual communities rather than the planner alone?
9. Is an exception labeled as fact, belief, proposal, or unknown?
10. Does the system create choices beyond combat?

Repair operation, access, and consequence before adding tiers, elements, or skill names.
