## 1. Organization versus setting label

A faction is not a color on a map or a shared personality. It is a decision structure pursuing a future with resources, routines, incomplete information, and internal conflict.

Store broad constitutional and social context in worldbuilding. Store a specific organization's membership, leadership, assets, procedures, current objectives, and decisions here.

## 2. Faction record

```text
Name, aliases, and who uses each name:
Status and source:
Public purpose:
Actual recurring work:
Future it is trying to create:
Legitimacy and constituency:
Membership and exclusions:
Decision-makers and succession:
Formal rules versus practical authority:
Resources, income, territory, records, and force:
Operational constraints and dependencies:
Internal blocs and disagreements:
External allies, rivals, clients, enemies, and obligations:
Current objectives and active operations:
Information held, distorted, delayed, or concealed:
Current losses, debts, scandals, and deadlines:
Linked characters, events, locations, items, and species:
Open uncertainty:
```

Do not fill every field for a temporary club or household group.

## 3. Decision structure

Trace how an idea becomes action:

```text
information arrives → someone interprets it → authority is claimed or granted
→ resources are assigned → intermediaries alter or delay the order
→ local execution → record, blame, reward, or concealment
```

Record vetoes, quorum, patronage, rank, expertise, informal influence, emergency powers, and communication delay only where they change events.

An organization may produce harmful outcomes without every member intending harm. Procedures, incentives, fear, and career survival can reproduce policy.

## 4. Resources and operations

Identify what the faction repeatedly needs and does:

- recruits, labor, experts, heirs, believers, customers, or informants;
- taxes, donations, dues, trade, rents, confiscation, grants, or plunder;
- food, medicine, weapons, documents, transport, housing, ritual material, and information;
- patrols, lessons, ceremonies, audits, research, production, relief, recruitment, propaganda, and punishment;
- maintenance work that continues between plot scenes.

Power without operations is decoration. Operations create points where characters can comply, bargain, exploit, fail, or resist.

## 5. Internal disagreement

Give major factions at least two credible pressures, not automatically two named camps:

- mission versus survival;
- central control versus local autonomy;
- purity versus recruitment;
- speed versus legitimacy;
- secrecy versus coordination;
- professional expertise versus political loyalty;
- present relief versus future capacity.

Members may agree on the goal and disagree on acceptable cost. Avoid making the “good faction” internally harmonious and the “bad faction” uniformly cruel.

## 6. External relationships

For each important relationship, record what moves between the parties:

- money, food, protection, legitimacy, labor, information, hostages, marriage, certification, territory, transport, or ritual recognition;
- public treaty versus private dependency;
- what each side thinks the other needs;
- what would trigger cooperation, defection, retaliation, or paralysis.

A faction may be an ally in one domain and a rival in another.

## 7. Organizational voice and information

Separate:

- official doctrine and public language;
- internal reports and professional vocabulary;
- local slang and practical shortcuts;
- leader preference;
- what ordinary members actually know;
- rumor and enemy interpretation.

Do not give every member the organization's full strategic knowledge. Orders may be obeyed for reasons different from those that produced them.

## 8. Faction continuity

After a relevant event, update only durable changes:

- leadership, membership, faction blocs, public legitimacy, policy, territory, resources, records, active operations, alliances, losses, and information state;
- who is blamed, promoted, indebted, silenced, or newly necessary;
- what routine continues despite the event.

A defeated army, church, sect, company, or court does not disappear merely because one leader falls. Track succession, fragments, records, dependents, and assets.

## 9. Common failures

- The organization thinks and speaks as one person.
- Its plan pauses when the protagonist leaves.
- Resources appear whenever a leader gives an order.
- Every subordinate knows the real objective.
- Internal disagreement exists only to reveal a traitor.
- Ranks and departments are names without different permissions or work.
- A conspiracy coordinates perfectly across distance and time.
- Institutions reform immediately after one speech or heroic act.
- Every faction has one good representative and one evil representative.
- The antagonist organization wants only to oppose the protagonist.

Repair decision flow, constituency, operations, and independent future before adding more ranks.
